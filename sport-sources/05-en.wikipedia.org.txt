The [[NFL playoffs|National Football League playoffs]] for the [[2025 NFL season|2025 season]] began on January 10, 2026, and concluded with [[Super Bowl LX]] on February 8 at [[Levi's Stadium]] in [[Santa Clara, California]] where the [[2025 Seattle Seahawks season|Seattle Seahawks]] defeated the [[2025 New England Patriots season|New England Patriots]] for the franchise's second title.
This is the first postseason since [[2014–15 NFL playoffs|2014]] not to feature the [[Kansas City Chiefs]]. {{Cite web|url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/chargers-eliminate-chiefs-from-postseason-contention-with-16-13-win|title=Chargers eliminate Chiefs from postseason contention with 16-13 win|last=Simmons|first=Myles|publisher=[[NBC Sports]]|quote=For the first time since 2014, the NFL postseason will not feature the Kansas City Chiefs.|date=December 14, 2025|accessdate=January 12, 2026}} Additionally, it marks the first postseason since [[1998–99 NFL playoffs|1998]] not to feature [[Peyton Manning]], [[Tom Brady]] or [[Patrick Mahomes]]. {{Cite web|url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/47543866/nfl-2025-playoffs-capsule-last-mahomes-brady-manning|title=Trends the last time Mahomes, Brady or Manning weren't in the playoffs|last=Gharib|first=Anthony|publisher=[[ESPN]]|quote=With the Kansas City Chiefs failing to qualify, this year marks the first postseason without Patrick Mahomes, Tom Brady or Peyton Manning since the 1998 campaign.|date=January 9, 2026|accessdate=January 12, 2026}}
It is also the first postseason since [[2022–23 NFL playoffs|2022]] to not feature either number one seed from the [[2024–25 NFL playoffs|previous season]] – the [[2025 Kansas City Chiefs season|Kansas City Chiefs]] and [[2025 Detroit Lions season|Detroit Lions]]. {{Cite web |date=December 26, 2025 |title=Lions playoff elimination: Explaining Detroit's rapid regression in 2025 |url=https://www.sportingnews.com/us/nfl/news/lions-playoff-2025-nfc-playoff-picture/944d442cb5d3b92973b0e2c3 |access-date=January 23, 2026 |website=sportingnews.com }} {{Cite web |date=December 21, 2025 |title=Is Chiefs' 2025 collapse a blessing in disguise? |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/47381191/chiefs-2025-season-collapse-patrick-mahomes |access-date=January 23, 2026 |website=ESPN.com }}
A total of 12 fourth-quarter lead changes occurred in the first four of the six wild card games played. These four games (Rams–Panthers, Packers–Bears, Bills–Jaguars and 49ers–Eagles) were decided by a winning touchdown inside the final three minutes. {{Cite web|url=https://apnews.com/article/nfl-playoffs-comebacks-a7337ef645d637f3d6149edcede61280|title=NFL playoff weekend sets a record with 12 fourth-quarter lead changes|last=Dubow|first=Josh|publisher=[[Associated Press]]|date=January 14, 2026|accessdate=January 14, 2026}}

This was the second playoff meeting between these two teams, following the Carolina Panthers' 29–23 win over the then-[[St. Louis Rams]] in the [[2003–04 NFL playoffs#NFC: Carolina Panthers 29, St. Louis Rams 23 (2OT)|2003 NFC Divisional Round]] in the opening seconds of the second overtime. {{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsi8PCMMhrY |title=Double OT Unreal Ending! (Panthers vs. Rams, 2003 Divisional Round) |date=January 6, 2026 |last=NFL Throwback |access-date=January 6, 2026 |via=YouTube}} This was the Panthers' first home playoff game since the [[2015 NFC Championship Game]], in which they beat the [[2015 Arizona Cardinals season|Arizona Cardinals]] 49–15 to reach [[Super Bowl 50]]. In Week 13 of [[2025 NFL season|this season]], the Panthers upset the Rams, 31–28. {{Cite web |title=Rams to face Panthers in Wild Card round of playoffs |url=https://www.therams.com/news/rams-to-face-panthers-in-wild-card-round-of-playoffs-2026 |access-date=January 5, 2026 |website=therams.com }} {{Cite web |title=Inside The Numbers: Panthers vs. Rams in Week 13 |url=https://www.panthers.com/news/inside-the-numbers-panthers-vs-rams-in-week-13-bryce-young |access-date=January 5, 2026 |website=panthers.com }}
The Panthers entered their first playoff game since the [[2017 Carolina Panthers season|2017 season]] as the largest home [[underdog]] in NFL playoff history. {{Cite web |last=Padula |first=Domenic |date=January 5, 2026 |title=Panthers tracking to be the biggest home underdog in NFL Playoff history versus Rams |url=https://www.tsn.ca/betting/article/panthers-tracking-to-be-the-biggest-home-underdog-in-nfl-playoff-history-versus-rams/ |access-date=January 9, 2026 |website=TSN }} [[Cam Newton]], the quarterback for the Panthers' last postseason game, banged the 'Keep Pounding' drum before the game. {{Cite news |last=Person |first=Joseph |date=January 11, 2026 |title=Cam Newton returns, gets Panthers-Rams started by pounding the drum |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6959930/2026/01/10/cam-newton-panthers-rams-pounding-drum/ |access-date=January 11, 2026 |work=The New York Times |issn=0362-4331}} The Panthers received the ball from the opening kickoff, but were stopped on their own 45-yard line after failing on fourth down. The Rams responded quickly with a 14-yard touchdown pass from [[Matthew Stafford]] to [[Puka Nacua]]. With 1:24 left in the first quarter, Rams cornerback [[Cobie Durant]] intercepted [[Bryce Young]] on the Rams' 44-yard line. After the pick, the Rams scored on a backwards pass to Nacua, who took it in for a 5-yard run. The Panthers responded with a 7-play, 65-yard drive that was capped by a 1-yard touchdown run from [[Chuba Hubbard]] to cut the score to 14–7, but a [[Harrison Mevis]] field goal on the next possession put the Rams up by 10. After forcing the Panthers to punt, Los Angeles then punted themselves, only for Panthers returner [[Trevor Etienne]] to muff the catch, allowing [[Troy Reeder]] to recover the ball on the Carolina 41-yard line. They managed to get into the red zone, but were stopped on fourth down at Carolina's 19-yard line. The Panthers made their way down the field thanks to a 28-yard pass interference penalty and a 37-yard completion to [[Jalen Coker]], before Bryce Young scrambled 16 yards into the end zone to reduce the deficit to 3 points with 44 seconds left in the half. The Panthers' ensuing kickoff fell short of the landing zone, giving the Rams the ball on their own 40-yard line. Stafford connected with [[Davante Adams]] to move the Rams just inside Carolina territory, but on third down from the 46-yard line, the usually sure-handed Nacua dropped a long pass that would have scored a touchdown; instead, the Rams had to punt and the Panthers knelt out the half with the score at 17–14.
After forcing the Rams to go three-and-out to open the second half, the Panthers tied the score at 17 thanks to a 46-yard field goal by rookie kicker [[Ryan Fitzgerald (American football)|Ryan Fitzgerald]] with 7:29 left in the third quarter. Aided by a 15-yard unnecessary roughness penalty by [[Nick Scott (American football)|Nick Scott]] for a helmet-to-helmet hit on Davante Adams, the Rams went ahead three minutes later on a 42-yard field goal by Mevis. Mike Jackson picked off Stafford after the Rams stopped the Panthers on fourth down near midfield. On the last play of the third quarter, Young connected with [[Jalen Coker]] on a 52-yard catch-and-run. Carolina scored a touchdown two plays later on a 3-yard run by Hubbard in the opening seconds of the fourth quarter to take a 24–20 lead. It was Hubbard's second rushing touchdown of the game after scoring just once in the regular season from the ground. The Rams immediately answered back with a 11-play, 67-yard drive on a Stafford 13-yard pass and run to [[Kyren Williams]] to go ahead 27–24. Plagued by [[American football positions|special teams]] mishaps all year, the Rams' punt was blocked on their own 30-yard line by [[Isaiah Simmons]]. A few plays later from the 7-yard line, Young found Coker in the back of the endzone to put the Panthers up 31–27 with 2:39 left. Stafford then drove the Rams down the field on a 71-yard drive that saw him pass on each of its seven plays, capped by a [[Colby Parkinson]] 19-yard sideline catch to take a 34–31 lead with 38 seconds left. This proved to be the game-winner, as the Panthers went four-and-out on their last possession of the game. {{Cite web |date=January 10, 2026 |title=Los Angeles Rams at Carolina Panthers 2025 POST 1 - Game Center |url=https://www.nfl.com/ |access-date=January 11, 2026 |website=NFL.com }}
Despite injuring a finger on his throwing hand and not completing a pass on seven straight passes from the second through the third quarter, {{Cite web |date=January 11, 2026 |title=Source: Stafford to have tests on injured finger |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/47580060/source-matthew-stafford-undergo-tests-injured-finger |access-date=January 11, 2026 |website=ESPN.com }} Stafford finished with 24 completions on 42 attempts, with 304 yards, three touchdowns and one interception; he also surpassed [[Kurt Warner]] for the most postseason passing yards and completions in Rams history. Nacua had 10 receptions for 111 yards and a touchdown. For the Panthers, Bryce Young went 21–40 on 264 yards with one touchdown and an interception in his playoff debut. Jalen Coker had 9 catches on 134 yards and a touchdown. Chuba Hubbard scored two touchdowns on the ground. {{Cite web |title=Rams 34-31 Panthers (Jan 10, 2026) Box Score |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/boxscore/_/gameId/401772979 |access-date=January 11, 2026 |website=ESPN }}

This was the third playoff meeting between the two longtime [[Bears–Packers rivalry|rivals]]. In the [[2010 NFC Championship Game]], the Packers defeated the Bears, 21–14, en route to their fourth [[Super Bowl XLV|Super Bowl championship]]. Chicago won the only prior meeting in the [[1941 NFL playoffs#Western Division championship|1941 NFL Western Division Championship]]. This was the rubber match between the [[NFC North]] rivals, as the Packers defeated the Bears in their first meeting on December 7 at [[Lambeau Field]] and the Bears returned the favor erasing a 10-point fourth-quarter deficit to beat the Packers on December 20 at [[Soldier Field]]. {{Cite web |title=Rubber match: Packers to play at Chicago in NFC Wild Card playoff |url=https://www.packers.com/news/rubber-match-packers-play-at-chicago-nfc-wild-card-playoff-2025-season |access-date=January 5, 2026 |website=packers.com }} {{Cite web |title=Game Recap: Bears battle back, but fall to Lions in regular-season finale |url=https://www.chicagobears.com/news/game-recap-bears-battle-back-but-fall-to-lions-in-regular-season-finale |access-date=January 5, 2026 |website=chicagobears.com }}
The Packers dominated the first half, scoring touchdowns on their first three offensive possessions. Quarterback [[Jordan Love]], returning from a concussion suffered against the Bears in Week 16, threw touchdown passes to [[Christian Watson]], [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Romeo Doubs]]. Green Bay's defense stifled Chicago's offense with an interception and two fourth-down stops, holding the Bears to a lone field goal. By halftime, the Packers held a 21–3 lead and had out-gained the Bears 231 to 122 in the first half.
The momentum started to shift in the third quarter. Chicago's defense forced a punt on each of Green Bay's first four second-half possessions. The Bears chipped away at the lead with two field goals from [[Cairo Santos]] and a 6-yard touchdown run by [[D'Andre Swift]], closing the gap to 21–16 with just over ten minutes remaining. Green Bay briefly halted the comeback when [[Matthew Golden]] scored a 23-yard touchdown on a screen pass. However, kicker [[Brandon McManus]] missed the extra point, leaving the score at 27–16. Chicago responded with an 8-yard touchdown strike from [[Caleb Williams]] to [[Olamide Zaccheaus]], followed by a successful two-point conversion to [[Colston Loveland]] to make the score 27–24.
With under three minutes remaining, McManus missed a 44-yard field goal that would have extended Green Bay's lead. Taking over with 2:51 left, Williams engineered a 6-play, 66-yard drive culminating in a go-ahead 25-yard touchdown pass to [[D. J. Moore]]. Green Bay reached the Chicago's 28-yard line on their final drive. As time expired, Love fumbled the snap and heaved a desperate pass into the end zone that was deflected by [[Kyler Gordon]], sealing the Chicago Bears' first playoff victory since 2011. {{Cite web |title=Bears 31-27 Packers (Jan 10, 2026) Game Recap |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/recap?gameId=401772981 |access-date=January 12, 2026 |website=ESPN }} The Bears outgained the Packers 323 to 190 in the second half.
For the Bears, it was their seventh fourth-quarter comeback of the season. Meanwhile, the Packers lost their fifth consecutive game. Their Week 16 game was considered one of the best games of the season. {{Cite web |last=Condotta |first=Bill |date=December 19, 2025 |title=Seahawks' rally vs. Rams was one of NFL's most unprecedented |url=https://www.seattletimes.com/sports/seahawks/seahawks-rally-vs-rams-was-one-of-nfls-most-unprecedented-four-downs/ |access-date=January 19, 2026 |website=The Seattle Times }} In addition, just one yard and one point separated the two teams this season in their two meetings. {{Cite web |last=DaSilva |first=Cameron |date=January 19, 2026 |title=Crazy stat shows how evenly matched Rams and Seahawks were this season |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/crazy-stat-shows-evenly-matched-051658721.html |access-date=January 19, 2026 |work=Yahoo Sports}} The Rams made their 12th [[NFC Championship Game#Appearances, 1970–present|NFC Championship Game]], and third since moving back to Los Angeles in [[2016 Los Angeles Rams season|2016]]. In the first playoff matchup between the #1 scoring offense (Rams) and #1 scoring defense (Seahawks) since the [[2014 NFC Championship Game]], {{Cite web |last=Smith |first=Michael David |date=2026-01-22 |title=Rams-Seahawks matches No. 1 scoring offense vs. No. 1 scoring defense |url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/rams-seahawks-matches-no-1-scoring-offense-vs-no-1-scoring-defense |access-date=2026-01-26 |website=NBC Sports}} the Rams started the game with the ball and went [[three-and-out]]. The Seahawks quickly scored a touchdown on a 7-play drive that took just 3:36 on a [[Kenneth Walker III]] 2-yard goal-line run. Over their next possessions, the Rams and Seahawks traded field goals to put the score at 10–3 at the end of the first quarter. Three minutes into the second quarter, Rams kicker [[Harrison Mevis]] kicked a 50-yard field goal to cut the Seahawks’ lead to four. The Rams then took a 13–10 lead on a 12-play, 87-yard, 7:35 drive on a [[Kyren Williams]] 9-yard pass from [[Matthew Stafford]]. [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] starred on the Seahawks’ final possession of the half, catching a 42-yard pass and 14-yard touchdown, to go back up 17–13. {{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2026-01-25 |title=Rams vs. Seahawks score, live updates: Seattle extends lead with TD after muffed punt by L.A. |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/live-blogs/rams-vs-seahawks-live-score-updates-nfl-playoffs-nfc-championship-game-result/a5u03hu9zlvB/ |access-date=2026-01-26 |website=The Athletic |language=en-US}}
The Seahawks' opening drive of the third quarter stalled after a holding penalty, but Rams punt returner [[Xavier Smith]] muffed the punt on Los Angeles' 17-yard line, which was recovered by Seattle. On the next play, [[Sam Darnold]] threw a 17-yard pass to [[Jake Bobo]] to go up 24–13. Stafford led the Rams down the field on just four plays, scoring a touchdown on a 4-yard pass to [[Davante Adams]]. It was Adams' tenth touchdown inside the 5-yard line this season. Former Ram [[Cooper Kupp]] caught a red zone pass from Darnold and ran it into the end zone for 13 yards on Seattle’s next possession to extend their lead to 11. Cornerback [[Riq Woolen]], who dropped a potential interception earlier in the drive, was called for a 15-yard taunting penalty, which kept a Rams drive that had stalled alive; on the ensuing play after the penalty, [[Puka Nacua]] shook Woolen to get open on a deep route and caught a 34-yard touchdown to cut into Seattle’s lead, 31–27, with 2:46 left in the third quarter. On the sidelines after the drive, Woolen and a few of his defensive teammates were seen arguing. {{Cite web |last=Williams |first=Charean |date=2026-01-26 |title=Riq Woolen's taunting penalty sets up Puka Nacua's touchdown |url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/riq-woolens-taunting-penalty-sets-up-puka-nacuas-touchdown |access-date=2026-01-26 |website=NBC Sports |language=en-US}} The Seahawks defense stepped up in the red zone on the Rams next drive by stopping them from scoring on a 7:24, 14-play drive, with the game now deep into the fourth quarter. On their final offensive drive, the Seahawks picked up multiple key first downs to force the Rams to use all of their timeouts. They still punted the ball back to the Rams with 31 seconds left, but Nacua could not get out of bounds on what turned out to be the last play of the game and the clock ran out. {{Cite web |title=Seahawks 31-27 Rams (Jan 25, 2026) Final Score |url=https://www.espn.com/nfl/game/_/gameId/401772987/rams-seahawks |access-date=2026-01-26 |website=ESPN |language=en}}
The Rams' weakness all season proved to be special teams and this was not rectified in time for the playoffs, which was exemplified when Smith muffed the punt at the start of the third quarter. {{Cite news |last=Rodrigue |first=Jourdan |date=2026-01-26 |title=The Rams had a Super Bowl team, but a glaring problem never went away |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6998847/2026/01/26/rams-lose-nfc-championship-game-special-teams/ |access-date=2026-01-28 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}} {{Cite web |last=Martin |first=Dan |date=2026-01-26 |title=Xavier Smith muffs Rams punt return as Seahawks score off blunder |url=https://nypost.com/2026/01/25/sports/xavier-smith-muffs-rams-punt-return-as-seahawks-score-off-blunder/ |access-date=2026-01-28 |language=en-US}} Darnold and Stafford both played at high levels. Darnold had three touchdown passes, 346 yards, and key throws to burn significant clock on the Seahawks’ last drive, {{Cite web |last=Owens |first=Jason |date=2026-01-26 |title=NFC championship: Seahawks ride late 4th-down stop, career game from Sam Darnold past Rams into Super Bowl |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/nfl/breaking-news/live/nfc-championship-seahawks-ride-late-4th-down-stop-career-game-from-sam-darnold-past-rams-into-super-bowl-220009240.html |access-date=2026-01-26 |website=Yahoo Sports |language=en-US}} while Stafford finished with 374 yards and three touchdowns of his own. Njigba caught 10 passes for 153 yards, scoring a touchdown just before the half. Nacua essentially matched him with 9 catches for 163 yards and a touchdown. Adams, on the losing end of his fifth NFC Championship Game, caught 4 catches for 89 yards and a touchdown. {{Cite web |last=Klein |first=Gary |date=2026-01-24 |title=After four heartbreakers, Davante Adams wants to make his Super Bowl dream a reality |url=https://www.latimes.com/sports/rams/story/2026-01-24/rams-davante-adams-wants-super-bowl-dream-reality |access-date=2026-01-26 |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}} Walker scored his fourth rushing touchdown of the playoffs and had 111 all-purpose yards.
