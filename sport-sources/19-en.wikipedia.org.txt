The '''2026 Giro d'Italia''' was the 109th edition of the [[Giro d'Italia]], a three-week [[Grand Tour (cycling)|Grand Tour]] cycling [[stage race]]. The race started on 8 May in [[Nesebar]], Bulgaria, and finished on 31 May in [[Rome]]. {{cite web|title=Giro d'Italia 2026 route features 40km time trial, return of the Passo Giau and 49,150 metres of climbing|url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/races/giro-d-italia-2026-route-features-40km-time-trial-return-of-the-passo-giau-and-49-150-metres-of-climbing/|first=Stephen|last=Farrand|website=cyclingnews.com|access-date=22 December 2025|language=en|date=1 December 2025}}
The race was won by Danish rider [[Jonas Vingegaard]] of {{UCI team code|TVL men|2026}} for the first time, becoming the eighth rider to win all three [[Grand Tour (cycling)|Grand Tours]] and the first to do so since [[Chris Froome]] in 2018. Vingegaard took the lead on stage 14, before slowly extending his lead to nearly five and a half minutes. Vingegaard won 5 stages, 4 of them in the mountains. {{Cite news |last=France-Presse |first=Agence |date=2026-05-31 |title=Vingegaard seals Giro d’Italia and is eighth cyclist to complete Grand Tour set |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/may/31/jonas-vingegaard-grand-tour-giro-d-italia-cycling |access-date=2026-06-01 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}
Second overall was Austrian rider [[Felix Gall]] of {{UCI team code|DCT|2026}}, with Australian rider [[Jai Hindley]] of {{UCI team code|RBH|2026}} third overall. In the other classifications, French rider [[Paul Magnier]] of {{UCI team code|SOQ|2026}} won the points classification, Italian rider [[Giulio Ciccone]] of {{UCI team code|LTK men|2026}} won the mountains classification, Portuguese rider [[Afonso Eulálio]] of {{UCI team code|TBV|2026}} won the young rider classification and Italian rider [[Manuele Tarozzi]] of {{UCI team code|BCS|2026}} won the sprints classification. The combativity award was won by Italian rider [[Damiano Caruso]] of {{UCI team code|TBV|2026|nolink=y}} and the team classification was won by {{UCI team code|TVL men|2026|nolink=y}}. {{Cite web |last=Ostanek |first=Dani |last2=Weislo |first2=Laura |date=2026-05-08 |title=Giro d'Italia final GC standings – No changes in Rome as Jonas Vingegaard celebrates overall victory |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/giro-d-italia-gc-standings-2026/ |access-date=2026-06-01 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
The race was followed by the [[2026 Giro d'Italia Women|2026 edition]] of the [[Giro d'Italia Women]], which had moved position in the calendar so not to clash with the men's [[Tour de France]]. {{Cite web |last= |first= |date=29 May 2026 |title=Giro d'Italia Women 2026: Everything you need to know |url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/giro-ditalia-women |access-date=2026-06-01 |website=Cycling Weekly |language=en}}
==Teams and cyclists==
{{Main|List of teams and cyclists in the 2026 Giro d'Italia}}
Twenty-three teams participated in the race. All 18 [[UCI WorldTeam|UCI WorldTeams]] were automatically invited. They were joined by five [[UCI ProSeries|UCI ProTeams]]. {{Cite web|title=Teams announced for the 2026 Giro d'Italia|url=https://www.giroditalia.it/en/news/teams-announced-for-the-2026-giro-ditalia/|date=20 February 2026|access-date=21 February 2026|website=Giro d'Italia 2026|language=en}}
Jonas Vingegaard ({{UCI team code|TVL men|2026}}) was considered to be the overall favourite to win the pink jersey. Other contenders for the podium and general classification included Jai Hindley and Giulio Pellizzari ({{UCI team code|RBH|2026}}), Egan Bernal ({{UCI team code|NCI|2026b}}), and Adam Yates ({{UCI team code|UEX|2026}}). {{cite web | title=Giro d'Italia 2026 form guide: Ranking the general classification contenders | website=[[Independent.co.uk]] | date=8 May 2026 | url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/cycling/giro-ditalia-2026-form-guide-contenders-vingegaard-b2966352.html }}
