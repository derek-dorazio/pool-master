The '''2026 Giro d'Italia Women''' was the 37th edition of the [[Giro d'Italia Women]], a women's [[Road bicycle racing|road cycling]] [[stage race]] in [[Italy]], and the 19th event of the [[2026 UCI Women's World Tour]] calendar. The race started on 30 May and finished on 7 June. The race was organised by [[RCS Sport]], which also organises the men's [[Giro d'Italia]].
The race moved to June from its traditional July date – and started on the same weekend as the finish of the men's race. {{Cite web |last=Mitchell |first=Mathew |date=2025-06-12 |title=UCI approves 2026 Women's WorldTour calendar and introduces key reforms for women's cycling |url=https://procyclinguk.com/uci-approves-2026-womens-worldtour-calendar-and-introduces-key-reforms-for-womens-cycling/ |access-date=2025-10-14 |language=en-GB|website=Pro Cycling UK}} The race previously had to compete for attention with the more famous men's {{Lang|fr|[[Tour de France]]|italic=no}}, and organisers had previously stated that they wished to move the calendar position of the race, so that the race is not overshadowed. {{Cite web |last=Rogers |first=Owen |date=10 July 2022 |title=Rome the target for 2023 Giro Donne 'grande partenza' |url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/rome-the-target-for-2023-giro-donne-grande-partenza |access-date=23 June 2023 |website=cyclingweekly.com |quote=Organisers confirm they are working with the UCI to ensure the race does not clash with the men's Tour de France}} Compared to other races in the UCI Women's World Tour, more ranking points are now awarded at the Giro d'Italia Women, [[Tour de France Femmes]] and the [[La Vuelta Femenina|Vuelta Femenina]] – elevating these races in status. {{Cite web |date=1 June 2025 |title=Part II Road Races - Amendments to Regulations as from 20.10.2025 |url=https://assets.ctfassets.net/761l7gh5x5an/NAeT9EN3grfH1b8uTXoYv/8b18929d380cb33fbb62d3efd3a71f27/2-ROA-20251020-E-amendments_on_20.10.25.pdf |access-date=20 June 2025 |website=[[Union Cycliste Internationale]] |page=15 |quote=2.10.017 Points scale Women Elite}} {{Cite web |last=Price |first=Matilda |date=2025-06-20 |title=UCI points update brings Women's WorldTour system in line with men's, designating women's Monuments and boosting Tour de France Femmes |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/news/uci-points-update-brings-womens-worldtour-system-in-line-with-mens-designating-womens-monuments-and-boosting-tour-de-france-femmes/ |access-date=2025-06-20 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
The race was won by Dutch rider [[Demi Vollering]] ({{UCI team code|FSF|2026}}) for the first time, after a strong performance on the final stage overcame a deficit of just under a minute. Vollering won two stages on her way to victory, becoming only the second woman after [[Annemiek van Vleuten]] to win all three [[Grand Tour (cycling)|Grand Tours]]. Second overall was German rider [[Antonia Niedermaier]] ({{UCI team code|CSZ|2026b}}), who performed strongly throughout the race, finishing in the top 5 on four stages. Third overall was Dutch rider [[Anna van der Breggen]] ({{UCI team code|SDW|2026}}), who led the race for four stages, after winning the mountainous [[individual time trial]] on stage 4. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-07 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Demi Vollering overtakes Anna van der Breggen on final day for overall title as Elisa Longo Borghini wins stage 9 sprint |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-demi-vollering-overtakes-anna-van-der-bregen-on-final-day-for-overall-title-as-elisa-longo-borghini-wins-stage-9-sprint/ |access-date=2026-06-07 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
In the race's other classifications, the mountains classification was won by Vollering, and the points classification was won by Italian rider [[Elisa Balsamo (cyclist)|Elisa Balsamo]] ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026}}), who won 4 stages. Her main rival [[Lorena Wiebes]] was disqualified from the race on stage 1 due to an underweight bike. The young rider classification was won by Canadian rider [[Isabella Holmgren]] ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}}), who finished 7th overall after finishing in the top 10 on four stages. {{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}} won the teams classification.

== Route and stages ==
[[File:ColleFinestre0001.jpg|thumb|Stage 8 finished at the top of [[Colle delle Finestre]], after an avalanche shortened the stage]]
In December 2025, the route was announced by organisers RCS Sport. {{Cite web |date=1 Dec 2025 |title=Giro d'Italia Women 2026: here is the route |url=https://www.giroditaliawomen.it/en/news/giro-ditalia-women-2026-here-is-the-route/ |access-date=2026-01-15 |website=Giro d'Italia Women 2026 |language=en}} It comprised nine days of racing with nine stages, covering a total of {{convert|1153.7|km|mi}} with {{convert|12,500|m|ft}} of elevation gain. The race started in [[Cesenatico]], [[Emilia-Romagna]] in northern Italy, before heading north through the [[Veneto]] and [[Friuli-Venezia Giulia]] regions, before heading west through the [[Trentino]], [[Lombardy]] and [[Piedmont]] regions. The race finished in [[Saluzzo]], Piedmont. {{Cite web |last=Price |first=Matilda |date=2025-12-01 |title=Giro d'Italia Women route to tackle epic Colle delle Finestre and steep uphill time trial in tough 2026 race |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-route-to-tackle-epic-colle-delle-finestre-and-steep-uphill-time-trial-in-tough-2026-race/ |access-date=2026-01-14 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite web |last=Camisa |first=Fulvia |title=Giro d'Italia Women 2026 route: The Colle delle Finestre makes its debut |url=https://www.rouleur.cc/blogs/the-rouleur-journal/giro-d-italia-women-2026-route-the-colle-delle-finestre-makes-its-debut |access-date=2026-01-14 |website=Rouleur |language=en}}
The race had two summit finishes, with an [[Individual time trial|uphill time trial]] between [[Belluno]] and {{Interlanguage link|Nevegal|lt=Nevegal|it}} in the Veneto region on stage 4, and a summit finish in [[Sestriere]] following the classic climb of [[Colle delle Finestre]] on stage 8. The Colle delle Finestre climb is {{convert|18.5|km|mi|abbr=on}} in length with an average gradient of 9.2%, with the final {{convert|8|km|mi|abbr=on}} of the climb on gravel roads. {{Cite web |title=Giro d'Italia Women 2026, a Thrilling Journey from Romagna to Saluzzo |url=https://www.giroditaliawomen.it/en/news/giro-ditalia-women-2026-a-thrilling-journey-from-romagna-to-saluzzo/ |access-date=2026-01-15 |website=Giro d'Italia Women 2026 |language=en}} As the highest climb of the race, the first rider to pass Colle delle Finestre ({{convert|2178|m}}) was awarded the "Cima Alfonsina Strada" – a prize named after Italian cyclist [[Alfonsina Strada]], who took part in the men's Giro d'Italia in 1924. {{Cite web |title=Giro d'Italia Women 2024: the route has been announced |url=https://www.giroditaliawomen.it/en/news/giro-ditalia-women-2024-the-route-has-been-announced/ |access-date=2024-01-31 |website=www.giroditaliawomen.it |language=en |quote='Cima Alfonsina Strada', the highest peak of the race, established in memory of the cyclist who took part in the men's Giro d'Italia in 1924.}}
The race returned to 9 stages in length, and was the longest edition of the Giro d'Italia Women since the {{Ill|Giro Donne 2001|lt=2001 edition|it}} of the race. As with previous editions, the route required a waiver from the [[Union Cycliste Internationale]], as Women's WorldTour races have a maximum race length of six days. {{cite web |last=Frattini |first=Kirsten |date=2021-10-16 |title=A closer look reveals the inequity at Tour de France Femmes |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/features/a-closer-look-reveals-the-inequity-at-tour-de-france-femmes/ |access-date=2022-02-28 |website=cyclingnews.com |language=en |quote=Regarding, the number of days of competition during a stage race, the UCI sets the elite women's stage races at six days, unless an exemption is made by its Management Committee.}}
Reacting to the route, [[Rouleur (magazine)|''Rouleur'']] stated that the "iconic climb" of Colle delle Finestre "makes clear just how demanding this route is" and that "pink jersey may not be decided until the very last minute" owing to the "mountainous back-loaded route". ''[[Cycling News]]'' called the route "really tough", summarising that a "lowkey start builds to a big crescendo with the Colle delle Finestre, a tough stage in the Dolomites, and a challenging uphill [time trial]". {{Cite web |last=Price |first=Matilda |date=2025-12-02 |title=A climber-friendly course that could get in the way of Elisa Longo Borghini's third title – Analysing the Giro d'Italia Women route |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/a-climber-friendly-course-that-could-get-in-the-way-of-elisa-longo-borghinis-third-title-analysing-the-giro-d-italia-women-route/ |access-date=2026-01-14 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} 2024 and 2025 winner [[Elisa Longo Borghini]] stated that she "really [likes] the route" and that "we needed an iconic climb like the Colle delle Finestre", and it "will decide the 2026 Giro Women". {{Cite web |last=Farrand |first=Stephen |date=2025-12-01 |title='The Colle delle Finestre will decide the 2026 Giro d'Italia Women' – Elisa Longo Borghini eyes third consecutive victory next summer |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/the-colle-delle-finestre-will-decide-the-2026-giro-d-italia-women-elisa-longo-borghini-eyes-third-consecutive-victory-next-summer/ |access-date=2026-01-14 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}

Prior to the start of the race, [[Demi Vollering]] ({{UCI team code|FSF|2026}}) was considered favourite for the overall victory. Other contenders for the general classification (GC) included 2024 and 2025 Giro d'Italia Women winner [[Elisa Longo Borghini]] ({{UCI team code|UAD women|2026a}}), four-time Giro d'Italia Women winner [[Anna van der Breggen]] ({{UCI team code|SDW|2026}}) and [[UCI Road World Championships – Women's time trial|world time trial champion]] [[Marlen Reusser]] ({{UCI team code|MOV women|2026}}). {{Cite web |last=Richardson |first=Simon |date=2026-05-29 |title=Who will win the Giro d'Italia Women? |url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/giro-ditalia/who-will-win-the-giro-d-italia-women |access-date=2026-05-31 |website=Cycling Weekly |language=en}} {{Cite web |last=Price |first=Matilda |date=2026-05-25 |title=Demi Vollering and Elisa Longo Borghini set to do battle, but super strong rivals could surprise them – Analysing the contenders for the Giro d'Italia Women |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/demi-vollering-and-elisa-longo-borghini-set-to-do-battle-but-super-strong-rivals-could-surprise-them-analysing-the-contenders-for-the-giro-d-italia-women/ |access-date=2026-05-31 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} ''[[Cycling News]]'' considered that the race would have "one of its biggest {{abbr|GC|general classification}} battles in recent history", owing to the greater gap between the Giro d'Italia Women and the Tour de France Femmes.

Wiebes was later disqualified from the race as her bike was {{Convert|20|g|lb}} under the weight limit of {{Convert|6.8|kg|lb}}. The race moved to the [[Veneto]] region, with the second stage finishing in [[Caorle]]. Balsamo won the stage in a sprint finish, retaining her lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, with a 20 second lead over most of the peloton thanks to bonus seconds. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-05-31 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Maglia rosa Elisa Balsamo speeds to second win in a row in stage 2 bunch sprint |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-elisa-balsamo-speeds-to-stage-2-sprint-win-in-maglia-rosa/ |access-date=2026-05-31 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
The third stage was a hilly course, finishing in [[Buja]] in the [[Friuli-Venezia Giulia]] region. On the climbs, GC contenders split the peloton, with a smaller group heading to the finish. In the uphill sprint to the finish line, Balsamo won her third stage ahead of [[Lily Williams (cyclist)|Lily Williams]] ({{UCI team code|HPH|2026|nolink=}}) and [[Femke Gerritse]] ({{UCI team code|SDW|2026|nolink=y}}). Balsamo maintained her lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, now around 30 seconds over most of the peloton. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-01 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Elisa Balsamo powers to hat-trick victory on stage 3 with searing uphill sprint |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-elisa-balsamo-powers-to-hat-trick-victory-on-stage-3-with-searing-uphill-sprint/ |access-date=2026-06-01 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
The fourth stage of the race was a {{convert|12.7|km|mi|abbr=on|adj=mid|-long}} [[individual time trial]] from [[Belluno]] to {{Interlanguage link|Nevegal|lt=Nevegal|it}} in the Veneto region. The uphill course included a {{convert|7.4|km|abbr=on}} climb with an average gradient of 8.2% and a maximum gradient of 14%. The fastest time of 31 minutes 38 minutes was set by Anna van der Breggen, with Marlen Reusser in second place, 1 minute 4 seconds behind. Demi Vollering finished third, six seconds slower. Van der Breggen therefore took the lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, with Balsamo falling to 56th overall. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-02 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Anna van der Breggen crushes opposition with stunning uphill time trial victory on stage 4, moves into pink jersey |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-anna-van-der-breggen-crushes-opposition-with-stunning-uphill-time-trial-victory-on-stage-4-moves-into-pink-jersey/ |access-date=2026-06-02 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} Van der Breggen last wore the ''maglia rosa'' of the leaders jersey on her way to victory at the [[2021 Giro Rosa|2021 edition]] of the race.
The fifth stage remained in the Veneto region, with a {{convert|138|km|abbr=on}} mountain stage in the [[Dolomites]] with 4 categorised climbs including the 1st category {{Interlanguage link|Passo Tre Croci|lt=Passo Tre Croci|it}}. On the last climb of the day, Vollering, van der Breggen, [[Antonia Niedermaier]] ({{UCI team code|CSR|2026b|nolink=}}) and [[Isabella Holmgren]] ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}}) formed a group of four riders. By the top of the climb, they had an advantage of 40 seconds over Longo Borghini and [[Niamh Fisher-Black]] ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}}), with Reusser 15 seconds further behind. Descending to the finish, the gap tightened however the group of four stayed away. Vollering took her first Giro d'Italia Women stage win by outsprinting van der Breggen and Niedermaier. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-03 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Demi Vollering takes sprint ahead of Anna van der Breggen for stage 5 win and first-ever career Giro stage victory |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-demi-vollering-takes-mountaintop-sprint-ahead-of-anna-van-der-breggen-for-stage-5-victory/ |access-date=2026-06-03 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} Van der Breggen maintained her lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, with Vollering gaining 10 seconds to move to second overall. Reusser finished 53 seconds behind Vollering, falling to fifth overall – thereby elevating Niedermaier moved to third overall.
The sixth stage of the race took riders south to [[Brescello]] in the [[Emilia-Romagna]] region on a flat route. Despite crosswinds on the stage, Balsamo won her fourth stage of the race in a sprint finish – further extending their lead in the points classification. Van der Breggen maintained her lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-04 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Fourth victory for Elisa Balsamo on stage 6 after 'best lead-out in the world' |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-fourth-stage-victory-for-elisa-balsamo-on-stage-6/ |access-date=2026-06-05 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
=== Stages in the mountains ===
The seventh stage was the longest of the race at {{convert|165|km|abbr=on}} in length, taking the riders westwards to [[Godiasco Salice Terme|Salice Terme]] in [[Lombardy]], with one climb at Pietragavina located {{convert|32|km|abbr=on}} from the finish. With {{convert|56|km|abbr=on}} to go, a large crash involved van der Breggen and Reusser, however they returned to the peloton and did not lose any time. On the descent from the Pietragavina climb, the breakaway included Longo Borghini, who aimed to gain time from her {{abbr|GC|general classification}} rivals. At the finish, the breakaway held off the chasing peloton with [[Célia Gery]] ({{UCI team code|FSF|2026|nolink=y}}) beating [[Lucinda Brand]] ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}}) and [[Chantal Pegolo]] ({{UCI team code|SBT|2026}}) in a sprint finish. Longo Borghini gained five seconds, but remained over 2 minutes behind van der Breggen. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-05 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Célia Gery wins stage 7 as Elisa Longo Borghini goes on the attack for time |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-celia-gery-wins-stage-7-as-elisa-longo-borghini-goes-on-the-attack-for-time/ |access-date=2026-06-05 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
The eighth stage was considered to be the ''[[queen stage]]'' of the race, originally planned to be {{convert|101|km|abbr=on}} from [[Rivoli, Piedmont|Rivoli]] to [[Sestriere]] in the [[Piedmont]] region. The stage would feature two climbs, the [[Colle delle Finestre]] ({{convert|18.5|km|mi|abbr=on}} in length with an average gradient of 9.2%, with the final {{convert|8|km|mi|abbr=on}} of the climb on gravel roads) and the ascent to the finish at [[Sestriere]].
On the climb to Colle delle Finestre, a {{abbr|GC|general classification}} group including Vollering, van der Breggen, Holmgren and Niedermaier slowly reduced the lead of the breakaway until they were at the front of the race. {{Cite web |last=Knöfler |first=Lukas |date=2026-06-06 |title=Giro d'Italia Women: Demi Vollering powers across gravel sector of Colle delle Finestre to win compressed stage 8 and second stage of race |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-demi-vollering-powers-across-gravel-sector-of-colle-delle-finestre-to-win-compressed-stage-8-and-second-stage-of-race/ |access-date=2026-06-06 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} With {{convert|7.5|km|abbr=on}} of the climb remaining, organisers announced that the stage would be shortened for safety reasons, finishing around {{convert|1|km|mi|abbr=on}} from the summit of the Colle delle Finestre. {{Cite web |last=Moultrie |first=James |date=2026-06-06 |title='It was absolutely unforeseeable' – Giro d'Italia Women queen stage shortened due to avalanche risk on Colle delle Finestre |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/womens-cycling/giro-d-italia-women-queen-stage-shortened-due-to-unstable-sheet-of-ice-that-could-fall-onto-the-road-with-finish-moved-to-1km-from-top-of-colle-delle-finestre/ |access-date=2026-06-06 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} Organisers noted an "unstable sheet of ice that could fall onto the road" and that the top of the climb had previously been blocked by an avalanche. The stage would therefore be {{convert|77.4|km|abbr=on}} in length. As riders were made aware of the shortened route, Vollering attacked several times with only van der Breggen, Holmgren and Niedermaier able to follow with around {{convert|5.5|km|abbr=on}} to go. After a flurry of attacks, van der Breggen set the pace at the front approaching the finish – with Vollering attacking in the final to take the stage win ahead of Holmgren and Niedermaier. Van de Breggen maintained her lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, however bonus seconds meant that her lead over Vollering had been cut to 49 seconds.
The ninth stage of the race started and finished in [[Saluzzo]] in the Piedmont region. The {{convert|143|km|abbr=on}} course included three categorised climbs, followed by a "punchy finale" into the finish in Saluzzo.
Niedermaier attacked with around {{convert|80|km|abbr=on}} to go, followed by Longo Borghini and Fisher-Black. Niedermaier gained a substantial lead of over two minutes, thereby taking the virtual lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}. On the Colletta di Brondello climb, Vollering attacked, distancing her rival van der Breggen and joining the group of Niedermaier, Longo Borghini and Fisher-Black with around {{convert|30|km|abbr=on}} remaining. Vollering had a lead over van der Breggen of over a minute, thereby taking the virtual {{abbr|GC|general classification}} lead. As the group of four pushed towards the finish, van der Breggen continued to lose time. Approaching the finish, Vollering knew overall victory had been secured, so let the other three riders fight for the win – with Longo Borghini beating a long sprint by Fisher-Black to take the stage win and secure 4th place overall. Van der Breggen finished the stage 2 minutes 23 seconds behind Vollering, and therefore fell to third overall behind Niedermaier.
=== Results ===
In the final general classification (GC), Demi Vollering ({{UCI team code|FSF|2026|nolink=y}}) won the Giro d'Italia Women for the first time, with an advantage over Antonia Niedermaier ({{UCI team code|CSZ|2026b|nolink=y}}) of 30 seconds. With her win, Vollering became the second woman after [[Annemiek van Vleuten]] to win all three [[Grand Tour (cycling)|Grand Tours]]. Third overall was Anna van der Breggen ({{UCI team code|SDW|2026|nolink=y}}), who was 1 minutes 37 seconds behind Vollering.
In the race's other classifications, the mountains classification was won by Vollering ahead of Niedermaier and van der Breggen. The points classification was led from start to finish by [[Elisa Balsamo (cyclist)|Elisa Balsamo]] ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}}), finishing with over twice as many points as her closest rival. The young rider classification was won by Isabella Holmgren ({{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}}), who finished 7th overall after finishing in the top 10 on four stages. {{UCI team code|LTK women|2026|nolink=y}} won the teams classification.
