The '''2026 NCAA Division I Men's Ice Hockey Tournament''' was the national championship tournament for [[National Collegiate Athletic Association]] (NCAA) men's [[NCAA Division I|Division I]] [[college ice hockey]] in the United States. Held from March 26 to April 11, 2026, the tournament involved a total 16 teams in [[single-elimination]] play to determine the national champion for the top level of play.
For the first time since [[1981 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|1981]], none of the four programs that compete in the annual [[Beanpot (ice hockey)|Beanpot]] tournament ([[Boston College Eagles men's ice hockey|Boston College]], [[Boston University Terriers men's ice hockey|Boston University]], [[Northeastern Huskies men's ice hockey|Northeastern]], and [[Harvard Crimson men's ice hockey|Harvard]]) qualified for the NCAA tournament. [[Dartmouth Big Green men's ice hockey|Dartmouth]] made its first tournament appearance since [[1980 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|1980]], ending the longest active drought between tournament appearances and tying [[Yale Bulldogs men's ice hockey|Yale]] for the longest gap between bids (46 years) in NCAA Division I men’s ice hockey history. As of 2026, the longest active drought since their last bid belongs to [[Alaska Anchorage Seawolves men's ice hockey|Alaska Anchorage]], who has not appeared in the tournament since [[1992 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|1992]] (34 years).
[[Michigan Wolverines men's ice hockey|Michigan]] earned a record-extending 29th Frozen Four appearance with their 4–3 win in the [[Albany, New York|Albany]] Regional Final over [[Minnesota Duluth Bulldogs men's ice hockey|Minnesota-Duluth]]. Also, [[Denver Pioneers men's ice hockey|Denver]] became the 6th program in NCAA Division I history to earn 20 Frozen Four appearances following their 6–2 win in the [[Loveland, Colorado|Loveland]] Regional Final over defending champions [[Western Michigan Broncos men's ice hockey|Western Michigan]].
Following their 4–3 overtime loss to [[Wisconsin Badgers men's ice hockey|Wisconsin]] in the [[Worcester, Massachusetts|Worcester]] Regional Final (a game in which they led 3–1 with five minutes remaining), [[Michigan State Spartans men's ice hockey|Michigan State]] became the first program in the 16-team tournament era (since [[2003 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|2003]]) to hold a 1-seed for 3 consecutive tournaments without reaching the Frozen Four.
This year’s Frozen Four featured a multiple overtime matchup for the second year in a row, with both games involving Denver. Following a 3-2 double overtime semifinal loss to Western Michigan in [[2025 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|2025]], Denver defeated Michigan 4-3 in a double overtime semifinal in 2026. Also in this year’s Frozen Four, Wisconsin became the first program in NCAA Division I history to advance to the National Championship game in seven straight Frozen Four appearances, with [[1978 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|1978]] being the last time Wisconsin advanced to the Frozen Four without appearing in the title game. Denver ultimately defeated Wisconsin 2-1 in the National Championship to earn their NCAA record-extending 11th title and become the first program since Boston College in [[2012 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament|2012]] to win three titles in five years.

The tournament was composed of four groups of four teams in regional brackets. The four regionals are named after the cities in which they take place. The following are the sites for the 2026 regionals: {{cite web|url=https://www.ncaa.com/championships/icehockey-men/d1/future-info |title=Future Dates & Sites |work=NCAA |accessdate=March 23, 2025}}

The puck see-sawed up and down the ice but both defenses were able to keep the attackers to the outside for the first several minutes. The first good scoring chance came about three and a half minutes in when [[Jayden Perron]] fired a low shot from the right circle but [[Lukas Swedin]] made the save with his leg. After a second chance for Michigan from [[T. J. Hughes (ice hockey)|T. J. Hughes]] was stopped at the 6-minute mark, Bentley got its first good look off of a turnover but [[Jack Ivankovic]] was able to stop the attempt from [[Arlo Merritt]]. After Bentley was able to put some pressure on the Michigan cage, Michigan countered with a rush and gave [[Luca Fantilli]] an open shot on goal. Swedin held firm and snagged the puck with his glove. The middle of the period saw Michigan continually attacking but Bentley's players collapsed back and prevented the Wolverines from setting up in the offensive zone. Just before the midway point of the period, Perron grabbed a clearing attempt with his glove just inside the Bentley zone. He then slid the puck over to Hughes who fired it under Swedin's glove for the game's opening marker. Bentley went on the attack on the ensuing faceoff and drew a hooking call on [[Dakoda Rheaume-Mullen]]. Despite some sloppy play from the 39th-ranked penalty kill, Michigan was able to limit Bentley to a low-percentage shots and retain their lead. After the penalty expired, the back and forth play resumed with the two sides limited to singular chances before the puck was either cleared or frozen for a faceoff. Both defenses played hard in their own zones while the attackers were looking more for good scoring chances than just trying to get the puck on goal. With less than 3 minutes left in the period, Michigan was finally able to establish itself in the Falcons' end, however, Bentley kept the Wolverines away from the goal and allowed Swedin to stop a long shot by [[Tyler Duke]] from the point. Just before the end of the period, [[Ben Robertson (ice hockey)|Ben Robertson]] was carrying the puck out of the Michigan end and he looked up at the clock to check the time. He then skated the puck forward and, as soon as he got to the Bentley blueline, he fired a hard wrist shot between two Falcon defenders. Swedin raised his glove to grab the puck but it sailed just below the outstretched trapper and into the net for a shocking goal, his first of the season.
Now down by 2, Bentley got right back to the attack at the start of the second period. Play again see-sawed between the two sides for the first few minutes but when [[Chase Davis (ice hockey)|Chase Davis]] attempted to collapse down low and help out defensively, he lost and edge and ran into Swedin. Michigan got possession of the puck while the cage was unoccupied but could not get the rubber into a shooting position before Swedin regain his footing. However, in order to prevent an open look at the goal, [[Tobias Larsen]] was forced to take a holding penalty. Michigan's #1 power play got several good shots on the Bentley cage but Swedin held firm and the Falcons were able to clear the puck and prevent a follow up chance. After the penalty expired, [[Jake Black (ice hockey)|Jake Black]] put a hard shot on goal off the rush. Ivankovic made the save but [[Oliver Salo]] crashed the crease and bodied the Michigan netminder to the ice. While the Wolverines called for a penalty, none was forthcoming. A few minutes later, Salo was handed a minor penalty when he was called for interference on [[Kason Muscutt]]. Michigan's offensive firepower was on full display on their second man-advantage. [[Will Horcoff]] and Perron had several good looks on goal but it was [[Nick Moldenhauer]] who found the back of the net after the Wolverines completely wore out the Bentley defenders. Bentley went on the attack once more, desperately trying to close the gap and managed to force a couple of offensive zone draws. Michigan, however, did not give the Falcons much to work with. On one of the Wolverines' counters up the ice, the puck was deflected into the corner behind the goal. Hughes jumped on the puck and backhanded out in front, sending the rubber into Muscutt's body where it rebounded towards the goal. While the puck probably would have entered the cage under its own power, Muscutt made sure by smacking it into the back of the net. After exchanging chances, Bentley got its chance on the power play when Duke was called for boarding. While most of the man-advantage went by the wayside, Bentley did get a tremendous scoring chance when Davis fired one on goal from the half wall. Ivankovic made the stop but the puck rebounded off of his stick right onto Salo's stick but the Finnish forward could not get his shot around the Michigan goaltender. Though they failed on their chance, the Falcons kept coming with both [[David Helledy]] and [[Garrett Horsager]] getting chances for a goal, however, Ivankovic stopped the former and a defensive wall provided by the Michigan skaters stopped the second. After a TV timeout, the Wolverines tilted the game back in their favor but Bentley would not go away. After Ivankovic stopped a [[Kellan Hjartarson]] shot, Black was nearly able to get his stick on the biscuit with a yawning cage in front but he overskated the puck. At time was counting down, [[Drew Schock]] broke towards the Bentley cage on a partial breakaway but Swedin made the save.
Bentley was again the aggressors at the start of the period but were still unable to get the puck past Ivankovic. However, less than a minute in the Falcons were able to force [[Cole McKinney]] into taking a holding call, granting them a third power play. Once again, the Falcons could not take advantage of Michigan's mediocre penalty kill and even surrendered a rush up the ice that saw Swedin having to stop a [[Garrett Schifsky]] backhand with his mask. Though Michigan was no longer pinching in the offensive zone, they were nonetheless able to get their shots thanks to Bentley being forced to push everything to the offense. Just before the 5-minute mark, Duke got mixed up behind his own goal with [[Michael Mesic]] and both were given roughing minors. The open ice gave both teams a chance to get the puck on goal but no goals were forthcoming. After the penalty box was emptied, Michigan continued to hold Bentley back until the Falcons iced the puck with just over 12 minutes to play. The Wolverines won the ensuing faceoff and Duke found Schifsky with a rink-wide pass and the junior fired the puck under Swedin right arm for Michigan fifth goal. Though the game was all but over by this point, Bentley refused to quit and went right at the Michigan cage. With under 10 minutes to play, Duke was called for holding and gave Bentley a fourth man-advantage. At the end of the power play, Salo put a long shot on goal that got behind Ivankovic but the Michigan goalie was able to cover the puck before any of the Falcons could follow up the attempt. [[Malcolm Spence (ice hockey)|Malcolm Spence]] had an opportunity to increase Michigan's lead with about 3 minutes to play but his shot hit a discarded stick and jumped up into Swedin's body. Bentley then did their best to finally get on the scoresheet but Ivankovic refused to budge. With 2 minutes left, Swedin was again tested and stopped a hard drive from Schock. In the final minute of play, Spence and Black were whistled for matching interference penalties. With under 30 seconds, Bentley finally broke through when Helledy fired a puck through Ivankovic, ending the [[shutout]] bid but the final result was unaffected.

| In the first interconference match of the tournament, the familiar foes had no need for a feeling out period. While trying to record the first goal, both sides played physically both before and after the many whistles in the early portion of the match. While neither side was able to find much rhythm, Wisconsin had the balance of play during the first half of the period. Michigan State began tilting the ice in their favor in the latter half of the period but neither side was able to get a grade-A scoring chance. It wasn't until about 3 minutes to play that Wisconsin was able to get a good shot at a goal off of a turnover, however, [[Trey Augustine]] was able to make a glove save on [[Jack Horbach]]. MSU got their chance with about a minute to play but [[Porter Martone]]'s attempt went wide.
Off of an offensive zone faceoff, [[Quinn Finley]] slid towards the center before firing a laser off the far post and into the net. MSU tried to set themselves up for an equalizer afterwards but the Wisconsin defense did not give them a decent opportunity. Martone got a bit overeager with trying to lay the body on the Badgers and was called for roughing about three minutes after the goal. Good passing a few miscues from Michigan State allowed the Badgers to get several shots on goal but Augustine stopped all six attempts on the man-advantage. Shortly after killing off the penalty, Horbach was called for high-sticking and MSU had its turn on the power play. Despite possessing one of the strongest special teams in the nation, MSU looked like they were about to wast their first chance when a shot from [[Gavin O'Connell]] deflected off of [[Ben Dexheimer]]'s stick and fluttered over [[Daniel Hauser (ice hockey)|Daniel Hauser]], into the net. Moments later, after confirming that #1 center [[Charlie Stramel]] had ben ruled out for the remainder of the game, [[Tommi Männistö]] found [[Patrick Geary (ice hockey)|Patrick Geary]] streaking towards the net with a backhand pass from below the goal line. The defenseman slammed the puck through Hauser for the Spartans' first lead of the game. Wisconsin then went on attack but the Spartans repaid the favor by holding the Badgers to the outside. With about six and a half minutes to play, [[Ryker Lee]] was handed an interference penalty to give Wisconsin a second shot with their power play. The red squad was far less competent on their second chance, hardly getting a shot on goal. To make matters worse, found Lee with a pass as he was exiting the box and gave the Spartan forward a breakaway. Houser made the initial save with his left pad but Lee celebrated after he saw the rubber vanish beneath the goalie's leg. The referee initially ruled that the puck did not cross the line but called for the play to be reviewed. After a second look at the play, the officials did not see the puck cross the line and let the play stand as called. With about 2 minutes to play, Michigan State nearly got their third goal when the puck hit Houser's glove and bounced behind the net. The netminder lost sight of the puck and when MSU moved the biscuit back in front for a follow up shot, the attempt hit Houser. As the rubber was rolling towards the net, the goaltender batted it away with his blocker as he was falling to the ice.
The second period began with MSU getting a great chance from the slot but [[Joe Palodichuk]] blocked the puck before it could get on goal. Wisconsin began to press but even when they got an offensive zone draw or a turnover, they were unable to turn it into much of a chance. At the 8-minute mark, Martone got a partial break and forced Dexheimer into a hooking call to stop his scoring chance. After seeing a few attempts go wide, [[Matt Basgall]] fired a shot from the blueline that sailed through a sea of bodies, off of the post and into the net. After the goal, Michigan State continued to pressure Badger puck carriers up and down the ice, not allowing Wisconsin a good shot on goal. Even when Wisconsin was able to get a puck on goal, Augustine was quick to cover the puck and end any potential rally before it could begin. However, Wisconsin kept shooting and with under 5 minutes to go they finally got a bounce to go their way. After a shot rebounded off the back boards, [[Oliver Tulk]] put a shot on goal from in tight. The puck deflected left to the stick of [[Luke Osburn]] and he threw it over Augustine's right leg for a goal. Moments later, Wisconsin pressure caused a similar play when Dexheimer found [[Christian Fitzgerald]] alone in front with a cross-crease pass. Fitzgerald's shot hit Augustine and bounced to the right and [[Gavin Morrissey]] smacked it home to tie the game. With exactly 3 minutes to play, [[Aiden Dubinsky]] was called for roughing, however, Wisconsin was saved from having to kill off a penalty due to [[Daniel Russell (ice hockey)|Daniel Russell]] receiving an embellishment minor. Wisconsin was almost able to get a high-percentage shot in the waning minutes but two separate passes eluded Badger sticks and time expired before the winning goal could be found.
It took Wisconsin just 24 seconds to earn their trip to the Frozen Four. After the Badgers rimmed the puck in and MSU failed to clear, Dexheimer threw a shot on goal from the point. [[Tiernan Shoudy]] attempted to grab the puck out of the air but instead deflected it down into his own cage.
