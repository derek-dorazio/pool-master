The '''2026 ATP Tour''' is the global elite men's professional [[tennis]] circuit organized by the [[Association of Tennis Professionals]] (ATP) for the 2026 tennis season. {{cite web|url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/what-is-the-2026-atp-tour-calendar|title=What is the 2026 ATP Tour calendar?|date=27 November 2025| accessdate=14 December 2025|website=ATP Tour|language=en}} The 2026 ATP Tour calendar comprises the [[Grand Slam (tennis)|Grand Slam]] tournaments, supervised by [[World Tennis]], the [[ATP Finals]], the [[ATP Tour Masters 1000]], the [[ATP Tour 500|ATP 500]], the [[ATP Tour 250|ATP 250]], and the [[United Cup]] (organized with the [[Women's Tennis Association|WTA]]). Also included in the 2026 calendar are the [[Davis Cup]] (organized by World Tennis), [[Next Gen ATP Finals]], and [[Laver Cup]], none of which distribute ranking points. {{Cite web |title=2026 ATP Tour calendar announced {{!}} ATP Tour {{!}} Tennis |url=http://www.atptour.com/en/news/2026-atp-tour-calendar-announced |access-date=2025-11-21|date=3 February 2025|website=ATP Tour|language=en}} {{cite news |title= 2026 ATP Tour Calendar|url=https://tennistourcalendar.com/2026-atp-tour-calendar/ |access-date=18 October 2025 |work=tennistourcalendar.com |date=22 March 2025 |language=en}}

The players/nations are sorted by:
# Total number of titles (a doubles title won by two players representing the same nation counts as only one win for the nation);
# Cumulated importance of those titles (one Grand Slam win equalling two Masters 1000 wins, one undefeated ATP Finals win equalling one-and-a-half Masters 1000 win, one Masters 1000 win equalling two 500 events wins, one 500 event win equalling two 250 events wins);
# A singles > doubles > mixed doubles hierarchy;
# Alphabetical order (by family names for players).

On 16 April 2026, Bautista Agut announced he would retire from professional tennis at the end of the season. {{cite web|url= https://nostresport.com/roberto-bautista-agut-anuncia-su-retirada-en-2026/|title=Roberto Bautista Agut announces his retirement in 2026|publisher=NostreSport|website=nostresport.com|date=16 April 2026|access-date=16 April 2026|lang=es}} {{cite web|url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/roberto-bautista-agut-retirement-news-2026|title= Bautista Agut: ‘I feel the time has come to start saying goodbye’ |website=ATPTour|date= 16 April 2026}} His final appearance was at the Copa del Rey de Tenis in July. {{cite web|last=Portoles|first=Miguel|url=https://www.lasprovincias.es/deportes/tenis/roberto-bautista-dice-adios-tenis-profesional-anos-20260713143440-nt.html|title= Roberto Bautista dice adiós al tenis profesional a sus 38 años| website=[[Las Provincias]]|date= 13 July 2026|accessdate=13 July 2026|lang=es}}

He made his final professional appearance at [[2026 Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]]. {{cite web|url= https://www.lta.org.uk/news/2026/june/dan-evans-set-for-retirement-after-wimbledon/ |title=Dan Evans set to retire after Wimbledon |publisher=LTA|website=lta.org.uk|date=11 June 2026|access-date=11 June 2026}}

Ram retired from professional tennis in September 2026, having made his final appearance at the [[2026 US Open – Men's doubles|2026 US Open]]. {{cite web|url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/us-open-2026-doubles-sf-ram-last-match|title=Ram & Salisbury fall in US Open SFs, marking Ram's final match|website=ATPTour|date= 11 September 2026}}

* {{flagicon|GBR}} '''[[Joe Salisbury]]''' joined the professional tour in 2014 and reached career-high rankings of No. 1 in doubles in April 2022. He won 17 career doubles titles, including four major titles in men's doubles at the [[2020 Australian Open – Men's doubles|2020 Australian Open]] and three consecutive US Open titles in [[2021 US Open – Men's doubles|2021]], [[2022 US Open – Men's doubles|2022]] and [[2023 US Open – Men's doubles|2023]], and two major titles in mixed doubles at the [[2021 French Open – Mixed doubles|2021 French Open]] and [[2021 US Open – Mixed doubles|2021 US Open]], all won while partnering [[Rajeev Ram]] and [[Desirae Krawczyk]] respectively. Salisbury retired from professional tennis in September 2026, having made his final appearance at the [[2026 US Open – Men's doubles|2026 US Open]]. {{cite web|url=https://www.lta.org.uk/news/2026/september/joe-salisbury-britains-most-successful-mens-doubles-player-announces-retirement/|title=Joe Salisbury: Britain’s most successful men’s doubles player announces retirement|publisher=[[Lawn Tennis Association|LTA]]|date=16 September 2026|accessdate=16 September 2026}}
* {{flagicon|SUI}} '''[[Stan Wawrinka]]''' joined the professional tour in 2002 and reached a career-high ranking of No. 3 in singles in January 2014. He won sixteen career singles titles, including three major singles titles at the [[2014 Australian Open – Men's singles|2014 Australian Open]], [[2015 French Open – Men's singles|2015 French Open]] and [[2016 US Open – Men's singles|2016 US Open]], and a [[Tennis at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Men's doubles|doubles gold medal]] at the [[2008 Summer Olympics]]. On 19 December 2025, Wawrinka announced that he will retire at the end of the season. {{cite web|url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/wawrinka-2026-retirement-announcement|title=Wawrinka announces 'one last push': 2026 will be his last year|publisher=ATPTour|website=atptour.com|date=19 December 2025|access-date=19 December 2025}}
* {{flagicon|ESP}} '''[[Bernabé Zapata Miralles]]''' joined the professional tour in 2015 and reached a career-high ranking of No. 37 in singles in May 2023. Zapata Miralles announced his retirement from professional tennis in December 2025, citing struggles with his mental health.
