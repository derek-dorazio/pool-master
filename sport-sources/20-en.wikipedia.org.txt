The '''2026 Milan–San Remo''' was a [[road bicycle racing|road cycling]] one-day race that took place on 21 March in north-western Italy. It was the 117th edition of the [[Milan–San Remo]] [[Classic cycle races|cycling classic]].
[[File:MilanoSanremo26.jpg|thumb|[[Tadej Pogačar]], [[Tom Pidcock]] and [[Mathieu van der Poel]] on the [[Cipressa]] climb]]
The race was won by [[Tadej Pogačar]] of {{UCI team code|UAD men|2026}} in a sprint finish against of [[Tom Pidcock]] of {{UCI team code|PQT|2026}}, after Pogačar attacked on the [[Cipressa]] climb for the second year in succession. Third place went to [[Wout van Aert]] of {{UCI team code|TVL|2026}}, the best of the chasing peloton behind.
Pogačar's win was his fourth different [[Cycling monument|Monument]] victory and was his first victory in the race after four consecutive top-five finishes. The victory marked Pogačar's eleventh in a Monument, tying [[Roger De Vlaeminck]]'s tally for the second-most Monument wins.

==Race==
The route of the 117th edition of Milan–San Remo was similar to recent years edition, with a start in [[Pavia]], just outside [[Milan]]. {{Cite web |last=Weislo |first=Laura |date=2026-03-04 |title=Milan-San Remo 2026 route |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/milan-san-remo-2026-route/ |access-date=2026-03-24 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} The route leading to [[Sanremo|San Remo]] remained largely intact, featuring iconic landmarks such as the [[Passo del Turchino]], the three Capi, the [[Cipressa]], and the pivotal [[Poggio di San Remo|Poggio]]. The overall race distance was {{Convert|298|km|mi}}.
Like most recent editions, the race came to life on the lead-up to the penultimate climb of [[Cipressa]]. With {{convert|32.5|km|abbr=on}} left, a crash brought down several riders, including [[Tadej Pogačar]] ({{UCI team code|UAD men|2026}}) and [[Wout van Aert]] ({{UCI team code|TVL|2026}}) while the reigning winner, [[Mathieu van der Poel]] ({{UCI team code|ADC|2026}}), was also involved. Pogačar made it back to the peloton at the foot of the Cipressa before being brought to the front by his teammate [[Brandon McNulty]], who began to set a furious pace. {{cite web|url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/milan-san-remo-tadej-pogacar-strikes-in-closing-metres-to-hold-off-tom-pidcock-in-electrifying-two-rider-finish-and-earns-masterful-victory/|title=Milan-San Remo: Tadej Pogačar strikes in closing metres to hold off Tom Pidcock in electrifying two-rider finish and earns masterful victory|first=Dani|last=Ostanek|work=Cycling News|date=21 March 2026|access-date=21 March 2026}} {{cite web|url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/tadej-pogacar-outsprints-tom-pidcock-to-win-milan-san-remo-after-late-crash|title='I thought it was all over' – Tadej Pogačar outsprints Tom Pidcock to win Milan-San Remo after late crash|first=Joseph|last=Lycett|work=Cycling Weekly|date=21 March 2026|access-date=21 March 2026}}
Following a leadout by teammate [[Isaac Del Toro]], Pogačar made his move with {{convert|24|km|abbr=on}} to go and {{convert|2.5|km|abbr=on}} from the top of the Cipressa, bringing [[Tom Pidcock]] ({{UCI team code|PQT|2026}}) and van der Poel with him. The trio worked together to keep the peloton at bay before reaching the foot of the [[Poggio di San Remo|Poggio]] with an advantage of 10 seconds. On the climb, Pogačar successfully dropped van der Poel with his first attack. He attacked a few more times to shake off Pidcock but to no avail, with the duo reaching the top of the Poggio with a lead of around 20 seconds.
On the Via Roma, Pogačar started his sprint with {{convert|200|m|abbr=on}} left, managing to hold off Pidcock by half a wheel to win the race. Just four seconds behind them, van Aert, who attacked the peloton at the bottom of the Poggio descent, barely held off the peloton to take third place.
Interviewed after the race, Pogačar stated that he thought the crash meant that his chance of victory "was all over". {{Cite web |last=Lycett |first=Joseph |date=2026-03-21 |title='I thought it was all over' – Tadej Pogačar outsprints Tom Pidcock to win Milan-San Remo after late crash |url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/tadej-pogacar-outsprints-tom-pidcock-to-win-milan-san-remo-after-late-crash |access-date=2026-03-24 |website=Cycling Weekly |language=en}} Pogačar complimented Pidcock, stating he is "punchy [and] a really fast guy", noting that he was "a bit afraid when [Pidcock] let me go first" in the final sprint. Pidcock stated that "winning by such close margins, [but] also losing by such close margins, is what makes the times when you win better". {{Cite web |last=Moultrie |first=James |date=2026-03-23 |title=Tom Pidcock suggests Milan-San Remo victory would have been 'a bit of a fluke' as he makes quick switch to GC mode for Volta a Catalunya |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/teams-riders/tom-pidcock-suggests-milan-san-remo-victory-would-have-been-a-bit-of-a-fluke-as-he-makes-quick-switch-to-gc-mode-for-volta-a-catalunya/ |access-date=2026-03-24 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
Following the race, {{UCI team code|UAD men|2026|nolink=y}} mechanics revealed that Pogačar had won the race on a bike with a cracked frame and a rubbing brake, following his crash earlier in the race. {{Cite web |last=Shrubsall |first=James |date=2026-03-24 |title=Tadej Pogačar won Milan-San Remo on a cracked frame with a rubbing disc brake |url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/tadej-pogacar-won-milan-san-remo-on-a-cracked-frame-with-a-rubbing-disc-brake |access-date=2026-03-24 |website=Cycling Weekly |language=en}}
