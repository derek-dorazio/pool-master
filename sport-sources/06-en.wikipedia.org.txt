The '''2025 Major League Baseball postseason''' was the playoff tournament of [[Major League Baseball]] (MLB) for the [[2025 Major League Baseball season|2025 season]]. In each of the two leagues—[[National League (baseball)|National]] and [[American League|American]]—the three division winners and three wild card teams (the remaining teams with the best win-loss records) qualified for the postseason, for a total of twelve teams. First was the best-of-three-games [[Wild Card Series]], with the two division winners with the best records in each league getting a [[bye (sports)|bye]]. Next was the best-of-five-games [[Division Series]]. The victors advanced to the best-of-seven-games [[League Championship Series]] to determine the [[Pennant (sports)#Baseball|pennant]] winners, the champions of each league. These two teams played against each other in the [[2025 World Series]], which began on October 24. {{Cite web |last=Anderson |first=R.J. |title=2025 MLB playoff schedule: Dates set for full postseason, including World Series start |url=https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/2025-mlb-playoff-schedule-dates-set-for-full-postseason-including-world-series-start/ |work=CBS Sport |date=August 12, 2025 |access-date=August 29, 2025}}
In the [[American League]], the [[Toronto Blue Jays]] returned for the fourth time in the last six years ([[2020 Toronto Blue Jays season|2020]], [[2022 Toronto Blue Jays season|2022]]–[[2023 Toronto Blue Jays season|2023]], 2025). Joining them were the [[New York Yankees]], who made their eighth appearance in the last nine years ([[2017 New York Yankees season|2017]]–[[2022 New York Yankees season|2022]], [[2024 New York Yankees season|2024]]–2025). The [[Seattle Mariners]] returned for the second time in the last four years ([[2022 Seattle Mariners season|2022]], 2025), and was their first postseason appearance as a division champion since [[2001 Major League Baseball postseason|2001]], ending the second-longest division title drought in the majors. {{Cite web |last=Kramer |first=Daniel |title=Party like it's 2001! Mariners clinch first AL West crown in 24 years |url=https://www.mlb.com/news/mariners-win-2025-al-west-division-title |website=[[MLB.com]] |date=September 25, 2025 |accessdate=September 25, 2025}} {{Cite web|url=https://calltothepen.com/2023/05/05/last-season-mlb-team-won-divisional-title-since-1994/|last=Tapp|first=Jerry|title=The last season each MLB team won a divisional title (since 1994)|work=Call To The Pen|date=May 25, 2023|accessdate=September 25, 2025}} Also returning were the [[Boston Red Sox]], who made their first appearance since [[2021 Major League Baseball postseason|2021]] and the sixth in the last thirteen years ([[2013 Boston Red Sox season|2013]], [[2016 Boston Red Sox season|2016]]–[[2018 Boston Red Sox season|2018]], 2021, 2025). {{Cite web |last=Browne |first=Ian |title=Rafaela launches walk-off triple as Red Sox punch playoff ticket |url=https://www.mlb.com/news/red-sox-clinch-2025-playoff-berth |website=MLB.com |date=September 26, 2026 |access-date=September 26, 2025}} The [[Detroit Tigers]] made their second consecutive postseason appearance ([[2024 Detroit Tigers season|2024]]–2025) {{Cite web |last=Beck |first=Jason |title=Tigers punch playoff ticket, still in hunt for division title |url=https://www.mlb.com/news/tigers-clinch-2025-playoff-berth |website=MLB.com |date=September 27, 2025 |access-date=September 27, 2025}} , and the [[Cleveland Guardians]] returned for the eighth time in the last thirteen years ([[2013 Cleveland Indians season|2013]], [[2016 Cleveland Indians season|2016]]–[[2018 Cleveland Indians season|2018]], [[2020 Cleveland Indians season|2020]], [[2022 Cleveland Guardians season|2022]], [[2024 Cleveland Guardians season|2024]]–2025). {{Cite web |last=Stebbins |first=Tim |title=Walk-off HBP (!) sends Guardians to postseason, punctuating remarkable run |url=https://www.mlb.com/news/guardians-clinch-2025-postseason-berth |date=September 27, 2025 |access-date=September 27, 2025}}
In the [[National League (baseball)|National League]], the [[Milwaukee Brewers]] clinched their seventh postseason berth in the last eight years ([[2018 Milwaukee Brewers season|2018]]–[[2021 Milwaukee Brewers season|2021]], [[2023 Milwaukee Brewers season|2023]]–2025). Joining them were the [[Philadelphia Phillies]], who returned for the fourth year in a row ([[2022 Philadelphia Phillies season|2022]]–2025). The [[Chicago Cubs]] made their first postseason appearance since [[2020 Chicago Cubs season|2020]] and their sixth appearance in the last eleven years ([[2015 Chicago Cubs season|2015]]–[[2018 Chicago Cubs season|2018]], 2020, 2025). Also returning were the [[Los Angeles Dodgers]], who made their thirteenth straight appearance ([[2013 Los Angeles Dodgers season|2013]]–2025), tying the New York Yankees for the second longest postseason appearance streak in MLB history (the Yankees previously made thirteen straight appearances from [[1995 Major League Baseball postseason|1995]] to [[2007 Major League Baseball postseason|2007]]). Next up were the [[San Diego Padres]], who returned for the fourth time in the last six years ([[2020 San Diego Padres season|2020]], [[2022 San Diego Padres season|2022]], [[2024 San Diego Padres season|2024]]–2025), {{Cite web |title=MLB playoff tracker 2025: Clinching scenarios, bracket, schedule |url=https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/46081635/mlb-2025-playoff-tracker-clinch-scenarios-postseason-bracket-schedule |website=ESPN.com |date=September 14, 2025 |accessdate=September 14, 2025}} and lastly were the [[Cincinnati Reds]], who made their first postseason berth since [[2020 Cincinnati Reds season|2020]] (and first in a full season since [[2013 Cincinnati Reds season|2013]]), marking the first time since then that both teams from [[Ohio]] made it to the postseason.
This was the first edition of the postseason since [[2016 Major League Baseball postseason|2016]] to not feature the [[Houston Astros]], who previously made eight straight appearances from [[2017 Major League Baseball postseason|2017]] to [[2024 Major League Baseball postseason|2024]], and the first since [[2014 Major League Baseball postseason|2014]] to not feature either team from [[Texas]]. {{Cite web|last=Baer|first=Jack|title=Astros eliminated from playoff contention, will miss postseason for first time since 2016|url=https://sports.yahoo.com/mlb/breaking-news/article/astros-eliminated-from-playoff-contention-will-miss-postseason-for-first-time-since-2016-015830325.html|work=Yahoo Sports|date=September 27, 2025|access-date=September 28, 2025}} This was also the first postseason since [[2017 Major League Baseball postseason|2017]] to not feature the [[Atlanta Braves]], who previously made seven straight appearances from [[2018 Major League Baseball postseason|2018]] to 2024.
The postseason began on September 30, 2025, and ended on November 2, 2025, with the [[Los Angeles Dodgers]] defeating the [[Toronto Blue Jays]] in the World Series. The Dodgers repeated as World Series champions and won their ninth championship. They became the eighth franchise in MLB history to win back-to-back titles. {{Cite web|last=Adler|first=David|title=Every back-to-back World Series champ|url=https://www.mlb.com/news/back-to-back-world-series-champions-c297636124|website=MLB.com|date=6 November 2021|accessdate=3 November 2025}}
The 2025 postseason was one of the most competitive in recent MLB history, with only two sweeps occurring across the entire bracket. Two Division Series, as well as the ALCS and World Series, all went to winner-take-all elimination games. It was also the first time during the Wild Card Series era (post-2021) that three of the four series went to a decisive Game 3. {{Cite web |title=2025 Postseason History |url=https://www.mlb.com/postseason/history/2025 |access-date=2026-01-05 |website=MLB.com |language=en}}

This was the second postseason meeting in the history of the [[Guardians–Tigers rivalry]]. They last met in the ALDS [[2024 American League Division Series#Cleveland vs. Detroit|the previous year]], which the Guardians won in five games. This time, the Tigers returned the favor, defeating the Guardians in three games to advance to the ALDS for the second straight year.
[[Tarik Skubal]] pitched {{fraction|7|2|3}} solid innings as the Tigers took Game 1. Game 2 remained tied through seven, then the Guardians put up five unanswered runs in the bottom of the eighth to even the series, capped off by a three-run home run by [[Bo Naylor]]. In Game 3, after the Guardians tied the score in the bottom of the fourth, [[Dillon Dingler]] hit a solo homer in the top of the sixth to put the Tigers ahead for good, and then the Tigers put up four more insurance runs in the top of the seventh to win 6–3 and advance. This was the Tigers’ first postseason victory over a team from their current division since winning the American League pennant in [[1984 ALCS|1984]].

This was the sixth postseason meeting in the history of the [[Yankees–Red Sox rivalry]] ([[1999 American League Championship Series|1999]], [[2003 American League Championship Series|2003]], [[2004 American League Championship Series|2004]], [[2018 American League Division Series|2018]], [[2021 American League Wild Card Game|2021]]). The Red Sox won the previous three meetings (2004, 2018, 2021). The Yankees defeated the Red Sox in three games to advance to the ALDS for the seventh time in nine years, marking their first postseason victory over the Red Sox since [[2003 ALCS|2003]].
[[Garrett Crochet]] pitched {{fraction|7|2|3}} solid innings as the Red Sox won Game 1. Game 2 remained tied after seven innings, until [[Austin Wells]] hit a two-out RBI single to put the Yankees ahead for good as they evened the series. In Game 3, [[Cam Schlittler]] pitched eight innings of shutout ball as the Yankees won 4–0 to advance, defeating their archrival in the postseason for the first time in 22 years. The Yankees became the first team to win their wildcard series, in its current three-game format, after losing the first game of the series. {{Cite web |last=Bourque |first=Delilah |date=2025-10-03 |title=Yankees Make MLB History With Wild Card Victory |url=https://www.si.com/mlb/yankees/news/new-york-yankees-mlb-history-wild-card-victory-red-sox |access-date=2025-10-08 |website=New York Yankees On SI |language=en-US}}
With the win by the Yankees, the playoff history between these two teams is tied at three series wins each.

In the top of the eleventh, [[Zach McKinstry]]'s RBI single put the Tigers in the lead for good, and [[Keider Montero]] shut down the Mariners in the bottom of the inning. In Game 2, [[Jorge Polanco]] hit two solo home runs off [[Tarik Skubal]], but [[Spencer Torkelson]] hit a two-run RBI double in the top of the eighth to tie the game. Crawford]], and [[Cal Raleigh]] all homered for the Mariners as they won by four runs to take the series lead. This was the sixth postseason meeting between the Phillies and Dodgers ([[1977 National League Championship Series|1977]], [[1978 National League Championship Series|1978]], [[1983 National League Championship Series|1983]], [[2008 National League Championship Series|2008]], [[2009 National League Championship Series|2009]]), and their first meeting outside of the NLCS. The Phillies won the most recent three meetings (1983, 2008, 2009). The Dodgers defeated the Phillies in four games to reach the NLCS for the tenth time in seventeen years.
In Game 1, the Phillies led 3–2 after six innings, then a three-run homer from [[Teoscar Hernández]] in the top of the seventh put the Dodgers in the lead for good, stealing a road win. Game 2 was a [[pitchers' duel]] between [[Blake Snell]] and [[Jesús Luzardo]], won by the former as the Dodgers took a 2–0 series lead headed to Los Angeles. In Game 3, the Phillies blew out the Dodgers to get on the board in the series. Game 4 was another pitchers' duel, which extended into extras, and after the Dodgers loaded the bases with two outs, pinch runner [[Hyeseong Kim]] scored on a walk-off error by Philadelphia's [[Orion Kerkering]] in the bottom of the eleventh to seal the series win.
This series was a repeat of the 1978 NLCS between both teams: just like in that series, the Dodgers stole the first two games on the road in Philadelphia, then the Phillies took Game 3 in a rout, and then the Dodgers won Game 4 in extra innings. With the win by the Dodgers, the playoff history between these two teams is tied at three series wins each.

This was the second postseason meeting between the Blue Jays and Mariners, the two [[1977 Major League Baseball expansion|AL expansion teams of 1977]]. They first met in the Wild Card series in [[2022 American League Wild Card Series#Toronto vs. Seattle|2022]], which the Mariners won in a sweep in Toronto. The Blue Jays overcame a two-games-to-none series deficit to defeat the Mariners in seven games, returning to the World Series for the first time since [[1993 World Series|1993]].
The Mariners continued their postseason winning streak at [[Rogers Centre]] to start the series. After a [[George Springer]] leadoff home run, the Mariners responded with three unanswered runs, which consisted of a home run by [[Cal Raleigh]] and two [[Jorge Polanco]] RBI singles for a 3–1 win in Game 1. {{Cite web |last=Castillo |first=Jorge |title=Miller sets tone for 'resilient' Mariners in G1 win |url=https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/46578125/mariners-shut-blue-jays-bats-steal-game-1-alcs |website=ESPN |date=October 13, 2025 |access-date=October 14, 2025}} The next day, [[Julio Rodríguez]], Polanco, and Naylor all hit multi-run home runs as the offense overcame [[Logan Gilbert]]'s three-inning, three-run start as the Mariners blew out the Blue Jays to take a 2–0 series lead headed to Seattle. {{Cite web |last=Clemens |first=Ben |date=October 14, 2025 |title=Gilbert's Gutsy Game and a Trio of Home Runs Give Mariners 2-0 ALCS Lead |url=https://blogs.fangraphs.com/gilberts-gutsy-game-and-a-trio-of-home-runs-give-mariners-2-0-alcs-lead/ |website=[[FanGraphs]] |access-date=October 14, 2025}} Things then took a turn for the worse for the Mariners, as the Blue Jays’ offense overwhelmed the Mariners pitching staff in back-to-back blowout wins to even the series at two. In Game 5, the Blue Jays took a 2–1 lead in the top of the sixth and were six outs away from taking the series lead, but then the Mariners put up five unanswered runs in the bottom of the eighth, capped off by a [[Grand slam (baseball)|grand slam]] from [[Eugenio Suárez]], to take a 3–2 series lead headed back to Toronto. In Game 6, home runs from [[Vladimir Guerrero Jr.]] and [[Addison Barger]] would help secure a 6-2 Blue Jays win as they forced a seventh game. In Seattle's first-ever Game 7, the Mariners led 3–1 in the bottom of the seventh and were eight outs away from winning their first AL pennant, but then Springer hit a three-run home run to put the Blue Jays in the lead for good, securing their first pennant in 32 years. This was the first Game 7 ever won by the Blue Jays in franchise history, and they became the first team to win a league pennant after losing the first two games at home.
Along with the [[Edmonton Oilers]] winning the [[2025 Stanley Cup playoffs#Western Conference final|NHL Western Conference title]], 2025 marked the first year since 1993 in which multiple Canadian teams were playing for a championship in the four major leagues, as the Blue Jays and the [[Montreal Canadiens]] previously won both the [[1993 World Series|World Series]] and [[1993 Stanley Cup Final|Stanley Cup]] respectively that year.
With the loss, the Mariners fell to 0–4 in the ALCS, having previously lost in [[1995 ALCS|1995]], [[2000 ALCS|2000]], and [[2001 ALCS|2001]].

This was the third postseason meeting between the Dodgers and Brewers (2018, [[2020 National League Wild Card Series#Los Angeles vs. Milwaukee|2020]]), and a rematch of the [[2018 NLCS]], which the Dodgers won in seven games before falling in the World Series. The Dodgers swept the overall top seed Brewers to return to the World Series for the second year in a row and fifth time in nine years.
[[Blake Snell]] pitched eight innings of shutout ball and struck out ten batters as the Dodgers took Game 1. {{Cite web |title=Snell spectacular and Dodgers barely hold on in 9th to beat Brewers 2-1 in NLCS opener |url=https://www.espn.com/mlb/recap/_/gameId/401809290 |website=ESPN.com |date=October 14, 2025 |access-date=October 17, 2025}} Game 1 was also notable for the Brewers pulling off the first 8-6-2 double play in postseason history despite the loss. {{Cite web |last=Rogers |first=Jesse |title=Brewers pull off 8-6-2 double play vs. Dodgers in NLCS Game 1 |url=https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/46588442/brewers-pull-wild-double-play-vs-dodgers-nlcs-game-1 |website=ESPN.com |date=October 13, 2025 |access-date=October 17, 2025}} In Game 2, [[Yoshinobu Yamamoto]] pitched a complete game as the Dodgers took a 2–0 series lead headed to Los Angeles. It was the first complete game in the postseason for the Dodgers since [[José Lima]] did it in the third game of the NLDS in [[2004 National League Division Series|2004]] and the first postseason complete game overall since [[Justin Verlander]] in Game 2 of the ALCS in [[2017 American League Championship Series|2017]]. Yamamoto was also the first Japanese pitcher to throw a complete game in the MLB postseason. {{Cite web |title=Yoshinobu Yamamoto pitches Dodgers to 2-0 NLCS lead vs. Brewers |url=https://africa.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/46599864/yoshinobu-yamamoto-pitches-complete-game-dodgers-win-game-2-nlcs-vs-brewers |website=ESPN.com |date=October 14, 2025 |access-date=October 14, 2025}} {{Cite web |last1=Stephen |first1=Eric |title=Yoshinobu Yamamoto throws first MLB postseason complete game by Japanese pitcher |url=https://www.truebluela.com/los-angeles-dodgers-news-notes/105915/dodgers-yoshinobu-yamamoto-complete-game-japan |website=True Blue LA |date=October 15, 2025 |access-date=October 15, 2025}} Game 3 remained tied until the sixth inning, when [[Tommy Edman]] put the Dodgers back in the lead for good with an RBI single, and then [[Freddie Freeman]] added an insurance run off a throwing error by [[Abner Uribe]] as the Dodgers took a commanding three games to none series lead. In Game 4, [[Shohei Ohtani]] carried the Dodgers to the pennant with three home runs, and pitching six shutout innings with ten strikeouts, completing the sweep.
With the win, the Dodgers improved their postseason record against the Brewers to 3–0, and it marked the first time since the 1970s that the Dodgers won at least three NL pennants in a single decade ([[1974 NLCS|1974]], [[1977 NLCS|1977]], [[1978 NLCS|1978]]).
Both teams met in the NLCS once again [[2026 NLCS|the next year]], where the Brewers returned the favor and swept the Dodgers.

This World Series marked the first meeting between teams from Toronto and Los Angeles in a postseason series since the [[1993 Stanley Cup playoffs#Clarence Campbell Conference final|1993 Stanley Cup playoffs]]. {{Cite web|url=https://jaysjournal.com/game-7-world-series-loss-another-addition-to-toronto-s-painful-sports-history-01k91g0xn39b|last=Ferraro|first=Jake|title=Game 7 World Series loss another addition to Toronto's painful sports history|website=Jays Journal|quote=Wayne Gretzky's controversial goal in 1993 lives forever in the minds of Maple Leafs fans and, ironically, was also a win for LA over Toronto.|date=November 2, 2025|accessdate=November 3, 2025}} In one of the closest and most grueling World Series ever played, the Dodgers narrowly defeated the Blue Jays in seven games to repeat as World Series champions, winning their ninth championship in franchise history.
In the first World Series game in Toronto in 32 years, the Blue Jays' offense chased [[Blake Snell]] from the mound in a Game 1 blowout win. Game 1 was also notable for [[Addison Barger]]’s pinch-hit grand slam in the bottom of the sixth, which was the first in World Series history. {{cite web|last=Matheson |first=Keegan |title=Grand moment! Barger belts first pinch-hit slam in World Series history|url=https://www.mlb.com/news/addison-barger-grand-slam-blue-jays-9-runs-6th-inning-world-series-game-1 |website=[[MLB.com]]|date=October 25, 2025|accessdate=October 25, 2025}} In Game 2, [[Yoshinobu Yamamoto]] pitched his second complete game of the postseason as the Dodgers prevailed by four runs to even the series headed to Los Angeles. Yamamoto became the first pitcher to throw multiple complete games in the same postseason since [[Madison Bumgarner]] in [[2014 Major League Baseball postseason|2014]], the first to do it in back-to-back games since [[Curt Schilling]] in [[2001 Major League Baseball postseason|2001]], and the first to throw a complete game in the World Series since [[Johnny Cueto]] in [[2015 World Series|2015]]. {{Cite web|last=Castrovince|first=Anthony|title=Yamamoto's gem, pair of solo shots carry Dodgers in Game 2|url=https://www.mlb.com/dodgers/news/dodgers-win-world-series-game-2-2025|website=[[MLB.com]]|date=October 25, 2025|accessdate=October 25, 2025}}
Game 3 was the most notable contest of the series. It was a long extra-innings grind which went eighteen innings, tying with Game 4 of the [[2005 National League Division Series#Atlanta vs. Houston|2005 NLDS]] (Atlanta vs. Houston), Game 2 of the [[2014 National League Division Series#Washington vs. San Francisco|2014 NLDS]] (Washington vs. San Francisco), Game 3 of the [[2018 World Series#Game 3|2018 World Series]] (which also featured the Dodgers), and Game 3 of the [[2022 American League Division Series#Houston vs. Seattle|2022 ALDS]] (Houston vs. Seattle) as [[Extra innings|the longest postseason games in MLB history in terms of innings]]. {{cite web|url=https://www.mlb.com/dodgers/news/dodgers-win-world-series-game-3-2025|title=Freeman does it again! Ends epic 18-inning game with walk-off HR |website=MLB.com |last=Castrovince |first=Anthony |date=October 28, 2025 |access-date=October 28, 2025}} After [[Shohei Ohtani]] forced extras with his second home run of the game in the bottom of the seventh, the game remained scoreless through the next ten and a half innings until the Dodgers prevailed off a [[Freddie Freeman]] walk-off solo homer in the bottom of the eighteenth. With his walk-off home run, Freeman was the first player to accomplish this feat in consecutive World Series, after his grand-slam walk-off in Game 1 of the [[2024 World Series]]. {{Cite web |last=Cassaval |first=AJ |title=5 takeaways from an epic Game 3 and a first look at Game 4 |url=https://www.mlb.com/news/takeaways-18-inning-world-series-game-3-2025 |website=MLB.com |access-date=October 28, 2025 |language=en}} Game 3 would ultimately be [[Clayton Kershaw]]’s final postseason game. {{Cite web|url= https://www.baseball-reference.com/players/gl.fcgi?id=kershcl01&t=p&post=1|title=Clayton Kershaw 2025 Pitching stats|website=Baseball Reference|accessdate=November 3, 2025}}
In Game 4, the Dodgers jumped out to an early lead, but it was quickly erased by a two-run homer by [[Vladimir Guerrero Jr.]] in the top of the second, which put the Blue Jays in the lead for good as they evened the series at two. In Game 5, [[Trey Yesavage]] pitched seven solid innings, and the Blue Jays' offense once again gave Snell trouble as they won 6–1 to take a 3–2 series lead headed back to Toronto. In Game 6, Yamamoto pitched six innings, allowing five hits and one run, and [[Mookie Betts]] got out of his postseason dry spell with a two-run RBI single in the top of the third as the Dodgers won to force a decisive seventh game. In Toronto's first ever World Series Game 7, the Blue Jays led 4–3 in the top of the ninth and were two outs away from their third title, but then [[Miguel Rojas (baseball)|Miguel Rojas]] hit a solo homer to tie the game, forcing the first extra inning Game 7 since [[2016 World Series#Game 7|2016]]. After a scoreless ninth and tenth, a solo homer from [[Will Smith (catcher)|Will Smith]] in the top of the eleventh put the Dodgers in the lead for good, and then Yamamoto earned his third win of the series in the bottom of the inning. This was the fifth consecutive World Series Game 7 to be won by the road team.
Yamamoto won World Series MVP, becoming the first Japanese-born player to accomplish such a feat since [[Hideki Matsui]] in [[2009 World Series|2009]]. {{Cite web |last=Anderson |first=R.J. |title=Yoshinobu Yamamoto named World Series MVP: Dodgers ace joins Randy Johnson with rare Game 7 feat |url=https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/yoshinobu-yamamoto-world-series-mvp-dodgers/ |website=[[CBS Sports]] |date=November 2, 2025 |access-date=November 2, 2025}} With the win, the Dodgers tied the [[Boston Red Sox]] and the [[Athletics (baseball)|Athletics]] for the third most World Series championships. The Dodgers became the first team to win back-to-back titles since the [[New York Yankees]] did so in [[1999 World Series|1999]] and [[2000 World Series|2000]] (as part of a World Series three-peat from [[1998 World Series|1998]] to 2000), and they became the first National League team to repeat as World Series champions overall since the [[Cincinnati Reds]] did so in [[1975 World Series|1975]] and [[1976 World Series|1976]]. The Dodgers also became the first team to win the World Series after sweeping the LCS, but with their opponent winning the pennant in a winner-take-all game since the [[Detroit Tigers]] did so in [[1984 World Series|1984]], and were the first to do it since the LCS moved to a seven-game series in [[1985 Major League Baseball postseason|1985]]. {{Cite web |last=Langs |first=Sarah |title=Here's why history is on Toronto's side |url=https://www.mlb.com/news/world-series-history-when-game-7-winner-faces-team-that-sweeps |website=[[MLB.com]] |date=October 21, 2025 |access-date=November 2, 2025}}
This was Toronto's first defeat in the World Series. Previously, they had won in their first two appearances in [[1992 World Series|1992]] and [[1993 World Series|1993]]. This marked the first time a Toronto-based team lost in a championship round of the four major North American leagues since the [[1960 Stanley Cup Final]]. {{Cite news |date=November 2, 2025|title=Congrats on the loss, Toronto. It was a masterpiece |url=https://www.theglobeandmail.com/sports/article-blue-jays-vs-dodgers-world-series-game-7-results-analysis-toronto/ |access-date=February 25, 2026|work=The Globe and Mail |language=en-CA}}
