The '''2026 Tour de France''' was the 113th edition of the [[Tour de France]]. The race began with a stage in [[Barcelona]], Spain, on 4 July, and finished with the final stage at the [[Champs-Élysées]], [[Paris]], on 26 July.
[[Tadej Pogačar]] of {{UCI team code|UAD men|2026}} won the [[General classification in the Tour de France|general classification]] for the fifth time, and the third year running. His attack on stage 6, at the [[Col du Tourmalet]] in the [[Pyrenees]], established a lead over his main rival [[Jonas Vingegaard]] of {{UCI team code|TVL men|2026}}. Pogačar extended his lead with victories on three mountain stages: stage 10 in the [[Massif Central]], stage 14 in [[Vosges]], and stage 19 to [[Alpe d'Huez]] in the [[French Alps]]. Pogačar's final margin of victory was almost six and a half minutes. It was the fastest race on record, with Pogačar having an average speed of {{Convert|43.227|km/h|mph}} over the three weeks of racing.
Second place overall went to [[Remco Evenepoel]] of {{UCI team code|RBH|2026}}, after Vingegaard withdrew from the race following a crash on stage 15. Evenepoel took his first mountain stage victory at [[Plateau de Solaison]] on stage 15 and then won the [[individual time trial]] to [[Thonon-les-Bains]] in stage 16. Third place went to [[Isaac del Toro]], a Tour debutant. On stage 20, Pogačar assisted his teammate Del Toro, to ensure that Del Toro held off fellow Tour debutant [[Paul Seixas]] of {{UCI team code|DCT|2026}} for third place overall and the [[Young rider classification in the Tour de France|young rider classification]] as the best rider under the age of 26.
The race was affected by a [[2026 European heatwaves|heatwave]], with two stages shortened and rules relaxed to allow for riders to stay hydrated. {{Cite web |last=Root |first=Tik |date=27 July 2026 |title=The year climate change came for the Tour de France |url=https://www.motherjones.com/environment/2026/07/the-year-climate-change-came-for-the-tour-de-france/ |access-date=27 July 2026 |website=Mother Jones |language=en-US}} Prior to stage 15, both Pogačar and Vingegaard were woken in the middle of the night to take [[Doping in cycling|doping tests]], which generated substantial debate. {{Cite web |date=19 July 2026 |title=Tour de France 2026: Stage 15 winner Remco Evenepoel criticises "inhuman" middle-of-the night doping tests |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/cycling/articles/c0jl84wp50wo |access-date=26 July 2026 |website=BBC Sport |language=en-GB}}
The [[Points classification in the Tour de France|points classification]] was won by [[Mads Pedersen]] of {{UCI team code|LTK men|2026}}. Pedersen won only one stage ([[2026 Tour de France, Stage 1 to Stage 11#Stage 4|stage 4]]), but consistently placed highly in the intermediate sprints and secured points in other sprint finishes. The [[Mountains classification in the Tour de France|mountains classification]] went to [[Richard Carapaz]] of {{UCI team code|EFE|2025}}, who won two mountain stages in the French Alps in the last week. Carapaz also won the award for the race's most combative rider. The [[Team classification in the Tour de France|teams classification]] was won by {{UCI team code|LTK men|2026|nolink=y}}.

The full route was announced on 23 October 2025 by [[Christian Prudhomme]], who described it as having a [[crescendo]] towards the finish. {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=23 October 2025 |title=Tour de France unveils 2026 route with double Alpe d’Huez for men and Ventoux debut for women |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2025/oct/23/tour-de-france-femme-2026-route-cycling |access-date=23 October 2025 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}} {{Cite web |last=Farrand |first=Stephen |date=23 October 2025 |title='It's a Tour in crescendo' – 2026 Tour de France designed to limit early Tadej Pogačar dominance |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/news/its-a-tour-in-crescendo-2026-tour-de-france-designed-to-limit-early-tadej-pogacar-dominance/ |access-date=24 October 2025 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} There were five summit finishes, including [[Plateau de Solaison]] for the first time and two finishes at Alpe d'Huez. The race featured only {{convert|26|km|abbr=on}} of [[individual time trial]], with a team time trial starting the Tour for the first time since [[1971 Tour de France|1971]]. The [[team time trial]] returned to the Tour for the first time since [[2019 Tour de France|2019]], and used new rules previously introduced at the [[2023 Paris–Nice]]. In this format, each rider is given an individual finish time, rather than all riders who finish together getting the same time. This format encourages tactical choices by general classification contenders – whether to ride with the rest of their team to save energy, or ride solo to achieve a better time.

Prior to the race, [[Tadej Pogačar]] and [[Jonas Vingegaard]] were widely considered to be the major contenders for the overall [[General classification in the Tour de France|general classification]] (GC), with the pair having won the [[Pogačar–Vingegaard rivalry|last six editions]] of the Tour between them. Leading up to the race, defending champion Pogačar won the [[2026 Tour de Suisse|Tour de Suisse]], and Vingegaard won the [[2026 Giro d'Italia|Giro d'Italia]]. Other riders considered contenders in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}} included [[Remco Evenepoel]], [[Florian Lipowitz]], [[Paul Seixas]] and [[Isaac del Toro]]. {{Cite web |last=Magnus |first=Emma |date=2 July 2026 |title=Tour de France 2026 preview: the contenders, sprinters and stage-hunters to watch |url=https://www.rouleur.cc/racing/tour-de-france-2026-preview-the-contenders-sprinters-and-stage-hunters-to-watch |access-date=2 July 2026 |website=[[Rouleur (magazine)|Rouleur]] |language=en |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260706145816/https://www.rouleur.cc/racing/tour-de-france-2026-preview-the-contenders-sprinters-and-stage-hunters-to-watch |archive-date=6 July 2026 |url-status=dead}} {{Cite web |last=Cash |first=Dane |date=29 June 2026 |title=Preview: Who can topple Tadej Pogačar at the 2026 Tour de France? |url=https://escapecollective.com/preview-who-can-topple-tadej-pogacar-at-the-2026-tour-de-france/ |access-date=2 July 2026 |website=Escape Collective |language=en}} {{Cite web |last=Strickson |first=Will |date=2 July 2026 |title=Cyclist’s Tour de France 2026 predictions |url=https://www.cyclist.co.uk/in-depth/cyclist-tour-de-france-2026-predictions |access-date=2 July 2026 |website=Cyclist |language=en}}
Top competitors for the green jersey of the [[Points classification in the Tour de France|points classification]] were expected to be former winners [[Jasper Philipsen]] and [[Biniam Girmay]], as well as [[Tim Merlier]] and [[Olav Kooij]]. Isaac del Toro and Paul Seixas were considered favourites for the white jersey of the [[Young rider classification in the Tour de France|young rider classification]].

Stage 6 was the first high mountain stage of the Tour, with five climbs in the Pyrenees, including the ''[[hors catégorie]]'' climb of [[Col du Tourmalet]] (17.1 km at an average gradient of 7.3%, with the summit at an elevation of {{convert|2115|m|ft|abbr=on}}). On the Tourmalet, {{UCI team code|UEX|2026|nolink=y}} set a fast pace, with Træen in the yellow jersey falling behind the main group with {{convert|11|km|abbr=on}} to go. With {{convert|5|km|abbr=on}} to the summit, del Toro attacked with Pogačar following. Vingegaard immediately began a solo chase, as the pace was too fast for Evenepoel, [[Paul Seixas]] ({{UCI team code|DCT|2026|nolink=y}}), and [[Florian Lipowitz]] ({{UCI team code|RBH|2026|nolink=y}}) to maintain. Pogačar made a solo breakaway with {{convert|43|km|abbr=on}} remaining, reaching the Tourmalet summit alone. Pogačar descended at pace, and pushed on to the uphill finish at [[Gavarnie-Gèdre]]. He won the stage by 2 minutes 38 seconds, ahead of a solo chase by Vingegaard. Other {{abbr|GC|general classification}} contenders including del Toro, Evenepoel, Seixas and Lipowitz finished a further 45 seconds behind. Pogačar took the yellow jersey of the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, with a lead of 2 minutes 42 seconds over Vingegaard, with del Toro (the new leader of the young rider classification) a further 45 seconds behind. Træen crashed on the descent from the Tourmalet, but continued after receiving medical attention; he finished around 30 minutes behind Pogačar. === Week two in the Massif Central, Vosges and Alps ===
[[File:2026-07-17_17-31-27_TDF-Belfort.jpg|thumb|[[Mauro Schmid]] and [[Harold Tejada]] approaching the finish line in [[Belfort]] on [[2026 Tour de France, Stage 12 to Stage 21#Stage 13|stage 13]]]]
After a rest day, the race continued in the Massif Central on [[Bastille Day]]. Pogačar attacked from the peloton on the first-category climb of [[Col du Perthus (Massif Central)|Col du Pertus]] (4.4 km at an average gradient of 8.5%) with around {{convert|15|km|abbr=on}} to the finish line. Overtaking [[Richard Carapaz]] ({{UCI team code|EFE|2026|nolink=y}}) prior to the summit, Pogačar rode to the finish line at [[Le Lioran]] to win his third stage of the Tour, extending his lead in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}} to over 3½ minutes. In the {{abbr|GC|general classification}} group behind, Evenepoel sprinted towards the line, finishing 32 seconds behind Pogačar and gaining a handful of seconds that moved him into third overall, ahead of [[Juan Ayuso]] ({{UCI team code|LTK men|2026|nolink=}}) who took the white jersey of the young rider classification. {{Cite web |last=Ostanek |first=Dani |date=14 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Tadej Pogačar shreds the field in the Massif Central to extend GC lead and claim stage 10 |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-tadej-pogacar-shreds-challengers-with-solo-climbing-charge-on-stage-10-and-adds-more-time-to-his-advantage-with-victory/ |access-date=24 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=14 July 2026 |title=‘I have haters’: Pogacar brushes off boos after solo burst extends Tour de France lead |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/14/joy-for-tadej-pogacar-on-bastille-day-as-solo-burst-extends-tour-de-france-lead |access-date=24 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}

Prior to the start of stage 15, the [[International Testing Agency]] woke Vingegaard around 2am and Pogačar around 5am, to administer [[Doping in cycling|drugs tests]] on them. Stage 15 took the race into the [[Savoy Prealps|Savoyard Alps]], with a finish at [[Plateau de Solaison]]. With about {{convert|24|km|mi|abbr=on}} remaining in the stage, Vingeegaard crashed after hitting the kerb while exiting a roundabout. He broke his collarbone and dropped out of the race. {{cite web|url=https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/tour-de-france/luckily-its-only-a-broken-collarbone-jonas-vingegaard-on-crashing-out-of-tour-de-france|title='Luckily, it's only a broken collarbone' – Jonas Vingegaard on crashing out of Tour de France|first=Adam|last=Becket|date=20 July 2026|accessdate=20 July 2026|website=cyclingweekly.com|publisher=[[Cycling Weekly]]}} {{Cite web |last=Rogers |first=Owen |date=19 July 2026 |title=Jonas Vingegaard crashes out of Tour de France – Dane suffers broken collarbone and will undergo surgery |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/teams-riders/jonas-vingegaard-crashes-out-of-tour-de-france/ |access-date=27 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} Merlier also dropped out during this stage, due to the challenging terrain. {{Cite web |last=Rogers |first=Owen |date=19 July 2026 |title=Three time stage winner Tim Merlier abandons the Tour de France on mountainous stage 15 |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/teams-riders/three-time-stage-winner-tim-merlier-abandons-the-tour-de-france-on-mountainous-stage-15/ |access-date=27 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} On the climb to the finish at Plateau de Solaison, Pogačar led from Del Toro and Evenepoel, as Seixas and Lipowitz lost time behind. In a sprint to the finish, Evenepoel finished ahead of Pogačar, winning his first mountain stage at the Tour. {{Cite web |last=Rogers |first=Owen |date=19 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Remco Evenepoel outwits Tadej Pogačar to win stage 15 as Jonas Vingegaard crashes out |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-remco-evenepoel-outwits-tadej-pogacar-to-win-stage-15-as-jonas-vingegaard-crashes-out/ |access-date=27 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} Pogačar speculated that Vingegaard's crash might have been due to lack of sleep following the drugs test.
=== Week three in the Alps ===
[[File:17e étape Tour de France 2026 - Maillots blanc et jaune au départ.JPG|thumb|{{UCI team code|UEX|2026|nolink=}} teammates [[Isaac del Toro]] (in the white jersey) and [[Tadej Pogačar]] (in the yellow jersey) prior to the start of [[2026 Tour de France, Stage 12 to Stage 21#Stage 17|stage 17]] in [[Chambéry]]]]
After the second rest day, the third week of the race began with a {{convert|26.1|km|abbr=on}} [[individual time trial]] to [[Thonon-les-Bains]]. Evenepoel set the fastest time, winning a Tour time trial stage for the third time in his career. Pogačar was second, 28 seconds slower, which reduced his GC lead to 4½ minutes. Third on the stage was [[Mattias Skjelmose]] ({{UCI team code|LTK men|2026|nolink=y}}), over a minute slower than Evenepoel. Seixas was faster than del Toro, cutting the gap between them for third in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}} to around 20 seconds. Lipowitz crashed on the descent, breaking his collarbone, causing him to drop out of the race. Ayuso therefore moved into fifth in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}}. {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=21 July 2026 |title=Evenepoel pips Pogacar again as Tour de France night-time doping test row deepens |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/21/remco-evenepoel-tadej-pogacar-tour-de-france-nightime-doping-test-row-cycling |access-date=27 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}} {{Cite web |last=Price |first=Matilda |date=21 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Time trial world champion Remco Evenepoel destroys race against the clock and takes back-to-back wins on stage 16 |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-time-trial-world-champion-remco-evenepoel-destroys-race-against-the-clock-and-takes-back-to-back-wins-on-stage-16/ |access-date=27 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
Stage 17 to [[Voiron]] was the last flat stage before three mountain stages in the Alps. Pedersen caught up with a large breakaway, then won the intermediate sprint at {{convert|28|km|abbr=on}}. Multiple attacks ensued, with a reduced group of sprinters approaching the finish. In the sprint finish, Philipsen used van der Poel to propel him to the stage victory, with Pedersen finishing in eighth place. Pedersen's lead in the points classification was cut to seven points, with few points still available before the Tour reached Paris. {{Cite web |last=Weislo |first=Laura |date=22 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Jasper Philipsen wins explosive sprint finish on stage 17 as points classification battle tightens |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-jasper-philipsen-wins-explosive-sprint-finish-on-stage-17-as-points-classification-battle-tightens/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=22 July 2026 |title=Anti-doping chiefs reject claims of Paul Seixas favouritism as Tour rivals cry foul |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/22/anti-doping-chiefs-reject-seixas-favouritism-claim-as-tour-de-france-rivals-cry-foul-cycling |access-date=28 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}
Stage 18 gradually climbed to a summit finish at [[Orcières-Merlette]] at an elevation of {{convert|1825|m|ft|abbr=on}}. A large breakaway contained strong climbers and Pedersen, who took maximum points at the intermediate sprint, extending his lead to thirty-two points. On the climbs, the breakaway group slowly eroded as [[Valentin Paret-Peintre]] ({{UCI team code|QST|2026|nolink=y}}) tried to gain points in the mountains classification. In the final climb, Carapaz broke away from the remaining four riders with {{convert|3.5|km|abbr=on}} to go, then took the stage win and the lead in the mountains classification. All the remaining {{abbr|GC|general classification}} contenders finished close together. {{UCI team code|UEX|2026|nolink=y}} were suffering from illness, which caused Pogačar's teammate [[Brandon McNulty]] to drop out of the race. {{Cite web |last=Ostinek |first=Dani |date=23 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Richard Carapaz flies solo across the final kilometres to land stunning mountaintop victory on stage 18 |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-richard-carapaz-flies-solo-across-the-final-kilometres-to-land-stunning-mountaintop-victory-on-stage-18/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=23 July 2026 |title=Tour de France worries emerge for Tadej Pogacar after illness sweeps through team |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/23/tour-de-france-stage-18-report-tadej-pogacar-team-illness-richard-carapaz |access-date=28 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}
Stage 19 to [[Alpe d'Huez]] was won by Pogačar, who attacked with {{convert|12|km|abbr=on}} to go on the climb. {{Cite web |last=Ostanek |first=Dani |last2= |first2= |date=24 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Tadej Pogačar flies to victory atop l'Alpe d'Huez on stage 19 |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-tadej-pogacar-flies-to-victory-atop-lalpe-dhuez-on-stage-19/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=24 July 2026 |title=Pogacar conquers Alpe d’Huez in record climb to win stage 19 and extend Tour de France lead |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/24/pogacar-wins-stage-19-alpe-d-huez-extends-tour-de-france-lead |access-date=28 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}} He finished in a record time for the Alpe d'Huez, beating Marco Pantani's time set in 1995. {{Cite web |last=Farrand|first=Stephen|date=24 July 2026|title=Tadej Pogačar smashes 30-year-old Alpe d'Huez climbing record set by Marco Pantani|url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/teams-riders/tadej-pogacar-beats-nearly-30-year-old-alpe-dhuez-climbing-record-set-by-marco-pantani/|access-date=27 July 2026|website=Cyclingnews|language=en}}
Stage 20 was the final mountain stage of the race and predicted to be the [[queen stage]]. It contained four climbs, three of them [[hors catégorie|''hors catégorie'']]. [[Sepp Kuss]] ({{UCI team code|TVL men|2026|nolink=y}}) was leading the stage after a breakaway, but he crashed twice in the final {{convert|8|km|abbr=on}}. Carapaz inherited the lead and won the stage, extending his lead in the mountains classification. Pogačar rode as a [[domestique]] for del Toro, helping the latter to secure third place in the {{abbr|GC|general classification}} and the lead in the young rider classification. Pedersen gained more points at the intermediate sprint, giving him an unassailable lead in the points classification. {{Cite web |last=Moultrie |first=James |date=25 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Richard Carapaz wins breakaway epic on brutal stage 20 to Alpe d'Huez after late disaster by Sepp Kuss |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-richard-carapaz-dominates-alpe-dhuez-with-second-win-in-as-many-days-after-late-disaster-by-sepp-kuss/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=25 July 2026 |title=Pogacar set for Tour de France glory in shortened finale as Carapaz wins stage 20 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/25/tadej-pogacar-tour-de-france-ricardo-carapaz-wildfires-cycling |access-date=28 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}
[[File:Étape_21_Tour_France_2026_Rue_Saint_Éleuthère_-_Paris_XVIII_(FR75)_-_2026-07-26_-_3.jpg|thumb|[[Mads Pedersen]], [[Tadej Pogačar]] and eventual stage winner [[Mathieu van der Poel]] on the [[Montmartre]] climb on [[2026 Tour de France, Stage 12 to Stage 21#Stage 21|stage 21]]]]
The final stage of the race was in [[Paris]], with the traditional [[Champs-Élysées stage in the Tour de France|final stage to the Champs-Élysées]]. The stage used the [[Montmartre]] circuit, previously used at the [[2024 Summer Olympics|Paris 2024 Summer Olympics]] and the [[2025 Tour de France]], modified to provide {{convert|11|km|abbr=on}} between the final ascent of Montmartre to the finish line on the Champs-Élysées. It was therefore predicted that sprinters would have a greater chance of contending for the stage win. {{Cite web |last=Lamoureux|first=Lyne|last2=|first2=|last3=|date=8 June 2026|title=Tour de France 2026 stage 21 preview|url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-2026-stage-21-preview/|access-date=5 July 2026|website=Cyclingnews|language=en}} The stage was shortened by {{convert|43.8|km|abbr=on}} due to redeployment of security staff to tackle [[2026 France wildfires|ongoing wildfires]] in [[Gironde]]. The {{abbr|GC|general classification}} was neutralized after the first Montmartre circuit, ensuring that crashes or other incidents would not impact the final {{abbr|GC|general classification}} standings. {{Cite web |last=Fletcher |first=Patrick |last2=Tyson |first2=Jackie Tadej |date=26 July 2026 |title=GC times to be taken before Montmartre climbs on Tour de France final stage for second year in a row due to safety concerns |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/gc-times-to-be-taken-before-montmartre-climbs-on-tour-de-france-final-stage-for-second-year-in-a-row-due-to-safety-concerns/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |date=25 July 2026 |title=Tour de France final stage to be shortened due to wildfires |url=https://www.lemonde.fr/en/sports/article/2026/07/25/tour-de-france-final-stage-to-be-shortened-due-to-wildfires_6755829_9.html |access-date=28 July 2026 |work=Le Monde |language=fr |issn=1950-6244}}
Pogačar attacked on the Montmartre climbs, attempting a breakaway with Evenepoel, van der Poel and Pedersen with one lap to go, but the group was caught by the peloton. On the final lap, Pogačar and van der Poel attacked again on the climb, but could only build a lead of 10 seconds ahead of the chasing peloton of sprinters. The pair worked together to maintain the gap on the descent and race to the line. The gap had narrowed to 5 seconds as they passed the [[Flamme rouge|''flamme rouge'']] with {{convert|1|km|abbr=on}} to go. On the final straight of the [[Champs-Élysées]], Pogačar was overtaken by van der Poel in a final push for the line. {{UCI team code|APT|2026|nolink=y}} led out Philipsen and {{UCI team code|LTK men|2026|nolink=y}} led out Pedersen, however van der Poel hung on to win the stage in a [[photo finish]], ahead of Philipsen and Pedersen. Pogačar finished in the chasing bunch of sprinters, sealing his fifth Tour victory. Carapaz, Pedersen and del Toro also finished the stage safely, winning their categories. {{Cite web |last=Moultrie |first=James |last2= |first2= |date=26 July 2026 |title=Tour de France: Tadej Pogačar equals record with fifth yellow jersey as Mathieu van der Poel wins thrilling stage 21 |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-tadej-pogacar-equals-record-with-fifth-yellow-jersey-as-mathieu-van-der-poel-wins-stage-21/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}} {{Cite news |last=Whittle |first=Jeremy |date=26 July 2026 |title=Relentless Tadej Pogacar wins fifth Tour de France to equal record |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jul/26/relentless-tadej-pogacar-wins-fifth-tour-de-france-to-equal-record |access-date=28 July 2026 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}
=== Results ===
In the [[General classification in the Tour de France|general classification]], Pogačar won the Tour for the fifth time. Only Pogačar, [[Jacques Anquetil]], [[Eddy Merckx]], [[Bernard Hinault]] and [[Miguel Induráin]] have five Tour victories. {{Cite news |last=Warwick |first=Matt |date=26 July 2026 |title=Pogacar wins fifth Tour as Van der Poel takes stage |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/cycling/articles/c07r8d4d2z5o |access-date=26 July 2026 |work=BBC News}} Pogačar's average speed of {{Convert|43.227|km/h|mph}} over the three weeks of racing was the fastest in the history of the Tour. {{Cite web |last=Giuliani |first=Simone |date=27 July 2026 |title=Tadej Pogačar's winning time sees 2026 Tour de France set new record for fastest-ever edition |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tadej-pogacars-winning-time-sees-2026-tour-de-france-set-new-record-for-fastest-ever-edition/ |access-date=27 July 2026 |website=Cycling News}} Evenepoel finished in second place, 6 minutes 26 seconds behind. Del Toro was third, 9 minutes 42 seconds behind Pogačar.
The [[Points classification in the Tour de France|points classification]] was won by Pedersen with 559 points, ahead of Philipsen and Girmay. Carapaz won the mountains classification, ahead of Pogačar and Paret-Peintre. Carapaz also won the award for the [[Combativity award in the Tour de France|most combative rider]]. The [[Young rider classification in the Tour de France|young rider classification]] was won by del Toro. The [[Team classification in the Tour de France|teams classification]] was won by {{UCI team code|LTK men|2026|nolink=y}}.
Of the 184 riders who started the race, 158 reached the finish in Paris. [[Cees Bol]] was the ''[[lanterne rouge]]'' in last place overall on {{abbr|GC|general classification}}, with an overall time around 6 hours and 20 minutes behind Pogačar. {{Cite web |date=4 July 2026 |title=Tour de France GC standings – No changes to top 10 as Tadej Pogačar puts on a show in Paris and confirms fifth title |url=https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/tour-de-france-gc-standings-2026/ |access-date=28 July 2026 |website=Cyclingnews |language=en}}
For the third time overall and for the first time since [[1999 Tour de France|1999]], French riders recorded no stage victories. {{Cite web |date=26 July 2026 |title=Who Won Stage 21 Of The Tour de France 2026?
