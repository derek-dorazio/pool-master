The '''2026 NASCAR Cup Series''' is the 78th season for [[NASCAR]] professional [[stock car racing]] in the United States and the 55th season for the modern-era Cup Series. The preseason featured the [[2026 Cook Out Clash|Cook Out Clash]] exhibition race on February 4 at [[Bowman Gray Stadium]], followed by the season-opening [[2026 Daytona 500|68th running]] of the [[Daytona 500]] on February 15 at [[Daytona International Speedway]]. The season will end at [[Homestead–Miami Speedway]] on November 8. {{cite web |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2025/08/20/nascar-releases-2026-schedule-adding-chicagoland-and-shifting-all-star-to-dover |title=NASCAR releases 2026 schedule, adding Chicagoland and shifting All-Star to Dover |website=[[NASCAR]] |date=August 20, 2025 |access-date=August 20, 2025}}
[[Kyle Larson]] of [[Hendrick Motorsports]] returns as the reigning NASCAR Cup Series champion. {{cite web |url=https://www.jayski.com/2025/11/02/kyle-larson-takes-cup-title-as-ryan-blaney-wins-shocker-in-sonoran-desert |title=Kyle Larson takes Cup title as Ryan Blaney wins shocker in Sonoran Desert |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |publisher=NASCAR Digital Media |date=November 2, 2025 |access-date=November 5, 2025}} Following the [[2026 Dollar Tree 301|Dollar Tree 301]] at [[New Hampshire Motor Speedway]], [[Denny Hamlin]] clinched the No. 1 seed in [[NASCAR Chase|The Chase]], marking its return as the Cup Series' championship format after last being implemented in [[2013 NASCAR Sprint Cup Series|2013]]. {{Cite web |last=Watson |first=Andrew |date=August 24, 2026 |title=Denny Hamlin claims the top seed in The Chase as three spots remain to compete for a title |url=https://wcyb.com/sports/nascar/denny-hamlin-claims-the-top-seed-in-the-chase-as-three-spots-remain-to-compete-for-a-title |access-date=August 24, 2026 |website=[[WCYB]]}}
This season was marred by the death of two-time Cup Series champion [[Kyle Busch]] due to [[sepsis]] caused by pneumonia on May 21, days before the [[2026 Coca-Cola 600|Coca-Cola 600]]. Busch was the first active full-time driver to pass away during a season since [[Dale Earnhardt]]'s death in [[2001 NASCAR Winston Cup Series|2001]], the first to do so off-track since [[Davey Allison]] in [[1993 NASCAR Winston Cup Series|1993]], and overall driver since [[Jason Leffler]], who was a part-time driver in [[2013 NASCAR Sprint Cup Series|2013]]. {{Cite web |last=Albert |first=Zack |date=May 21, 2026 |title=Kyle Busch, two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion, dies at age 41 |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/05/21/kyle-busch-two-time-nascar-cup-series-champion-dies-at-age-41/ |access-date=May 21, 2026 |website=[[NASCAR]]}}

===Team changes===
[[Chevrolet]] unveiled a new body style for the [[Chevrolet Camaro (sixth generation)#ZL1|ZL1]] in November 2025 for the 2026 season to replace the model that had been used since [[2022 NASCAR Cup Series|2022]]. The redesign is based on the Camaro ZL1 Carbon Performance Package accessories kit. {{cite web |url=https://www.jayski.com/2025/11/14/chevrolet-camaro-zl1-racecar-set-to-debut-new-look-for-2026-nascar-cup-series-season/ |title=Chevrolet Camaro ZL1 racecar set to debut new look for 2026 NASCAR Cup Series season |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |publisher=NASCAR Digital Media |date=November 14, 2025 |access-date=November 14, 2025}} [[Haas Factory Team]] and [[Rick Ware Racing]] switched from [[Ford Motor Company|Ford]] to Chevrolet in 2026. HFT has a technical alliance with [[Hendrick Motorsports]], while RWR formed a technical partnership with [[Richard Childress Racing]].
===Driver changes===
[[Justin Haley (racing driver)|Justin Haley]] did not return to [[Spire Motorsports]]. Haley returned to [[Kaulig Racing]], driving in the [[2026 NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series|Craftsman Truck Series]]. {{cite web |url=https://www.jayski.com/2025/10/29/kaulig-racing-adds-daniel-dye-and-justin-haley-to-ram-1500-roster |title=Kaulig Racing adds Daniel Dye and Justin Haley to RAM 1500 roster |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |publisher=NASCAR Digital Media |date=October 29, 2025 |access-date=October 29, 2025}} [[Daniel Suárez]], who was released from [[Trackhouse Racing]], moved to Spire Motorports in Haley's place.
[[Kyle Busch]] died three days before the [[2026 Coca-Cola 600]] from hemorrhagic shock caused by sepsis from untreated pneumonia; he was going to skip the race weekend to deal with the illness and [[Austin Hill]] was to drive in his place. In his honor, [[Richard Childress Racing]] decided to stop using his number, 8, and hold it until his son [[Brexton Busch|Brexton]] could begin racing in NASCAR; the car was renumbered to 33. On June 6, Hill was named as Busch's permanent replacement for at least the remainder of the season.
====Rookie====
{{See also|NASCAR Rookie of the Year#Cup Series}}
[[Connor Zilisch]] is the only full-time rookie for 2026. He signed with Trackhouse Racing to replace Daniel Suárez.
==Rule changes==
===Technical changes===
====Horsepower====
Horsepower increased from 670 to 750 at tracks under 1.5 miles and road courses. {{Cite web |title=Teams to run 750hp engines at road courses and tracks shorter than 1.5 miles in 2026 |url=https://www.jayski.com/2025/10/08/teams-to-run-750hp-engines-at-road-courses-and-tracks-shorter-than-1-5-miles-in-2026/ |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |publisher=NASCAR Digital Media |date=October 8, 2025 |access-date=October 8, 2025}}
====Track packages====
[[Bristol Motor Speedway|Bristol]], [[Darlington Raceway|Darlington]], [[Dover Motor Speedway|Dover]], [[Nashville Superspeedway|Nashville]], [[New Hampshire Motor Speedway|New Hampshire]], and [[Gateway Motorsports Park|St. Louis]] would run the short track package rather than the intermediate package from previous years. {{Cite web |title=NASCAR issues technical updates, specifying rules for 750-horsepower tracks, A-post flaps |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2025/11/14/nascar-2026-rule-book-technical-updates/ |website=[[NASCAR]] |date=November 14, 2025 |access-date=November 14, 2025}} NASCAR also mandated a permanent A-post flap for all races to prevent flips. The A-post flap debuted the previous year at [[2025 Coke Zero Sugar 400|Daytona II]]. {{ cite web |url=https://tobychristie.com/nascar/cup-series/nascar-makes-technical-changes-a-post-flap-now-mandatory |title=NASCAR Makes Technical Changes; A-Post Flap Now Mandatory |date=November 14, 2025 |access-date=November 15, 2025}}
==== Superspeedway packages ====
NASCAR changed the superspeedway package starting at [[2026 Coke Zero Sugar 400|Daytona II]] to a "4-inch spoiler and wide splitter stuffers. The spoiler deflection device was redesigned to accommodate the 4-inch spoiler. New spoiler braces and an engine tapered spacer were reduced to 27/32-inch." {{Cite web |date=July 15, 2026 |title=NASCAR announces superspeedway package changes |url=https://www.jayski.com/2026/07/15/nascar-announces-superspeedway-package-changes-for-daytona/ |access-date=July 15, 2026 |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]]}} Starting at [[2026 YellaWood 500|Talladega II]], the spoiler was shrunk again to "3.5 inches". {{Cite web |last=Page |first=Scott |date=October 1, 2026 |title=NASCAR makes rules package changes for Talladega race |url=https://www.jayski.com/2026/10/01/nascar-makes-rules-package-changes-for-talladega-race/ |access-date=October 1, 2026 |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |language=en-US}}
===Policy changes===
====Loosening lower series restriction====
The amount of races full-time Cup drivers can run in the [[NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series|O'Reilly Auto Parts Series]] and [[NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series|Craftsman Truck Series]] was increased to ten and eight respectively, up from the 2025 restriction of five in each series. Cup drivers remain ineligible for points, as well as unable to compete in the postseason events. {{cite web |url=https://www.jayski.com/2025/11/01/nascar-to-allow-cup-drivers-to-run-more-lower-series-races |title=NASCAR to allow Cup drivers to run more lower series races |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |publisher=NASCAR Digital Media |date=November 1, 2025 |access-date=November 15, 2025}}
====Age limit====
The minimum age requirement to race in the O'Reilly Auto Parts series was lowered to 17 for road courses and oval tracks less than 1.25 miles in length. This change creates a staggered minimum age: 16 in Trucks, 17 in O'Reilly, and 18 in Cup Series events.
==== Qualifying hand restriction ====
The restriction of hands out the window during qualifying at drafting tracks such as [[Daytona International Speedway|Daytona]], [[Talladega Superspeedway|Talladega]], and [[Atlanta Motor Speedway|Atlanta]] (EchoPark) was added, and drivers who touch the window net or the hole in between to redirect air will have their qualifying time disallowed. {{Cite web |date=February 7, 2026 |title=Drivers can no longer put hands out window during qualifying |url=https://www.jayski.com/2026/02/07/drivers-can-no-longer-put-hands-out-window-during-qualifying/ |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |access-date=February 7, 2026}} [[Noah Gragson]] was the first to break this rule, having his [[2026 Daytona 500|Daytona 500]] qualifying time erased. {{Cite web |date=February 12, 2026 |title=Gragson busted for violating new rule during Daytona qualifying |url=https://www.espn.com/racing/nascar/cup/story/_/id/47909424/gragson-busted-violating-new-rule-daytona-500-qualifying |website=ESPN.com |access-date=March 16, 2026}}
==== Increased practice time ====
Practice time was increased at most tracks besides [[Daytona International Speedway|Daytona]], [[Talladega Superspeedway|Talladega]] and [[Atlanta Motor Speedway|Atlanta]]. The change saw two groups of 25 minutes each to one 50-minute session involving all cars. {{Cite web |date=July 18, 2026 |title=NASCAR expected to double Cup Series practice time |url=https://www.jayski.com/2026/07/18/nascar-expected-to-double-cup-series-practice-time/ |access-date=July 19, 2026 |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]]}} Beginning at [[New Hampshire Motor Speedway]], Cup Series practice will be consolidated into a single 50-minute session. {{Cite web |date=July 22, 2026 |title=NASCAR to implement 50-minute Cup practices beginning at New Hampshire |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/07/22/nascar-to-implement-50-minute-cup-practices-beginning-at-new-hampshire/ |access-date=July 22, 2026 |website=[[NASCAR]]}}
==== Shorter caution periods ====
Beginning at [[2026 Iowa Corn 350|Iowa]], NASCAR announced that when a caution comes out with 10 laps or less in the stage, the stage will be deemed official. {{cite web |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/08/06/nascar-to-implement-new-stage-break-procedure-beginning-at-iowa-speedway/ |title=NASCAR to implement new stage-break procedure beginning at Iowa Speedway |website=[[NASCAR]] |date=August 6, 2026 |access-date=August 6, 2026}}
===Charter agreement changes===
Following the outcome of the [[23XI Racing v. NASCAR|23XI Racing lawsuit]], all teams were offered "evergreen" charters. In contrast to previous agreements, which expired at a set date, the new agreement would include charters that would not expire. {{Cite web |last=Albino |first=Dustin |date=December 11, 2025 |title=NASCAR reaches settlement with 23XI Racing, Front Row Motorsports |url=https://www.jayski.com/2025/12/11/nascar-reaches-settlement-with-23xi-racing-front-row-motorsports/ |website=[[Jayski's Silly Season Site]] |access-date=December 11, 2025}}
===Points system changes===
====Postseason format====
NASCAR revealed a new postseason format in January 2026. Used in the top three series, it is a return of the [[NASCAR Chase|Chase format]] previously used from [[2004 NASCAR Nextel Cup Series|2004]] to [[2013 NASCAR Sprint Cup Series|2013]]. The top 16 drivers on points after 26 races will qualify for the Chase, with the 'win-and-you're-in' rule being scrapped, and starting points in the Chase will be staggered based on the regular season standings. {{cite web |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/01/12/nascar-returns-to-chase-championship-format-for-2026/ |title=NASCAR returns to Chase championship format for 2026 |website=[[NASCAR]] |date=January 12, 2026 |access-date=January 12, 2026}} This format replaced a derided playoff system, which included four "rounds" with points resets. With the change, playoff points are no longer awarded.
====First-place point increase====
The points awarded for finishing first in a race was increased from 40 to 55.
====Fastest lap restriction====
Drivers who enter the garage during a race will no longer be eligible for the fastest lap bonus point. If the driver's fastest lap was achieved before they entered the garage, it will still stand. {{cite web |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/brucemartin/2026/01/16/nascar-announces-rules-updates-ahead-of-2026-season/ |title=NASCAR Announces Rules Updates Ahead Of 2026 Season |website=[[Forbes]] |date=January 16, 2026}}
==== Removal of regular season championship ====
With the changes of the chase, the regular season championship was removed. {{Cite web |last=Wilberforce |first=Dogli |date=July 22, 2026 |title=NASCAR Quietly Ends Long-Standing Championship Honor, and Not Everyone Will Like It |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/nascar-quietly-ends-long-standing-140949305.html |access-date=July 22, 2026 |website=[[Yahoo Sports]]}}

==Season summary==
===Preseason races===
Due to [[January 30 – February 2, 2026 nor'easter|a historic snowstorm]] over the United States, the [[2026 Cook Out Clash|Cook Out Clash]] was postponed three times from its original Saturday and Sunday dates, before ultimately being condensed to a one-day event on Wednesday, February 4. {{cite web |date=January 29, 2026 |title=Cook Out Clash Saturday events postponed at Bowman Gray Stadium |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/01/29/cook-out-clash-saturday-events-postponed-at-bowman-gray-stadium/ |website=[[NASCAR]]}} {{cite web |date=January 31, 2025 |title=Clash postponed to Monday |url=https://www.jayski.com/2026/01/31/cook-out-clash-postponed-to-monday/}} Reigning champion [[Kyle Larson]] won pole position. Weather plagued the event, causing NASCAR to mandate use of wet weather tires after a scheduled half-race break. After an event record 17 cautions, [[Ryan Preece]] won the Clash, joining [[Jeff Gordon]] and [[Denny Hamlin]] as drivers who won the Clash before winning their first points paying race. {{cite web |date=February 4, 2026 |title=Ryan Preece wins Cook Out Clash at Bowman Gray amid winter weather |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/02/04/cook-out-clash-recap-2026-ryan-preece-wins-bowman-gray/ |website=[[NASCAR]]}}
The [[2026 America 250 Florida Duel at Daytona|Duel at Daytona]] races set the starting order for the [[2026 Daytona 500|Daytona 500]], with the exception of the front row, which is set by the fastest two times in qualifying. [[Kyle Busch]] and [[Chase Briscoe]] won the pole and second place respectively, and therefore started first in each duel race. {{Cite web |author=Staff Report |date=February 13, 2025 |title=Busch wins pole for Daytona 500; Briscoe on front row for second straight year |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/02/11/kyle-busch-wins-pole-for-daytona-500-briscoe-on-front-row-for-second-straight-year/ |access-date=February 12, 2026 |website=[[NASCAR]]}} During the first Duel, [[RFK Racing]] dominated the majority of the race, seeking to help [[Corey LaJoie]] clinch among the "Open" cars, as he had not qualified for the race. Unfortunately for LaJoie, a last lap wreck ended his chances of making the 500. [[Casey Mears]], despite a spin earlier in the race, dodged LaJoie during the wreck, clinching his spot into the race, as well as making his first Daytona 500 start since [[2019 Daytona 500|2019's race]]. {{cite web |title=Logano, Elliott win Duel races; Mears, Alfredo lock into Daytona 500 |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/02/12/joey-logano-wins-first-duel-race-mears-locks-into-daytona-500/ |access-date=February 12, 2026 |website=[[NASCAR]]}}
During the second Duel, which ran caution-free, [[Chase Elliott]] finished 0.065 seconds ahead of [[Carson Hocevar]]. [[Anthony Alfredo]] finished in position to make the Daytona 500 as the last open car, but was disqualified after a post-race technical inspection found that his car's cooling hoses were not secured. Due to the disqualification, the next-best finishing open car, driven by [[B. J.
