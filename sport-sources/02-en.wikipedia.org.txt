The '''2027 FIA Formula One World Championship''' is a planned motor racing championship for [[Formula One cars]] which will be the 78th running of the [[Formula One World Championship]]. It is recognised by the [[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile]] (FIA), the governing body of international [[motorsport]], as the highest class of competition for [[open-wheel racing car]]s. The championship is scheduled to be contested over twenty-four Grands Prix held around the world. Drivers and teams are set to compete for the titles of [[List of Formula One World Drivers' Champions|World Drivers' Champion]] and [[List of Formula One World Constructors' Champions|World Constructors' Champion]], respectively.
== Entries ==
The following constructors and drivers are under contract to compete in the 2027 World Championship. All teams are due to compete with tyres supplied by [[Pirelli]]. {{cite news |title=Pirelli to continue as Formula 1's exclusive tyre supplier until 2027 |url=https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/pirelli-to-continue-as-formula-1s-exclusive-tyre-supplier-until-2027.7xJIxJyMe84N3p7k4iIMjK |access-date=30 April 2024 |work=Formula1.com |date=10 October 2023 |language=en}} Each team is required to enter at least two drivers, one for each of the two mandatory cars.

== Regulation changes ==
===Technical regulations===
The engine regulations introduced in {{F1|2026}} will be amended to increase internal combustion engine power from 400kW to 420kW while reducing maximum electrical power deployment in the MGU-K from 350kW to 300kW. This changes the ratio of power from 53:47 to 58:42, before changing to 60:40 in 2028. {{cite web
|title=FIA's WMSC ratifies F1 regulatory changes for 2027 and 2028
|url=https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/fias-wmsc-ratifies-f1-regulatory-changes-for-2027-and-2028.ACyRcPvBKo8LRiOleCSJU
|website=Formula 1
|date=23 June 2026
|access-date=13 September 2026
}} As a result, fuel flow will increase 5%, which in turn has required overall race distance to be shortened from {{cvt|305|km|mi|abbr=on}} to {{cvt|290|km|mi|abbr=on}} to allow teams to continue to use elements of their 2026 chassis designs which can include the fuel tank. {{cite web
|title=F1 Grand Prix distances set to be reduced for 2027 season along with introduction of 'hard stop' time on race days
|url=https://www.skysports.com/f1/news/13590496/f1-grand-prix-distances-set-to-be-reduced-for-2027-season-along-with-introduction-of-hard-stop-time-on-race-days

In addition to engine changes, gearbox homologation will also be scrapped starting in 2027, allowing teams to alter their design in-season as they see fit. {{cite web|url=https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/regulatory-changes-for-2026-and-2027-agreed-at-latest-f1-commission-meeting.2wFfaMinLUT40dRVw3FeCC|title=Regulatory changes for 2026 and 2027 agreed at latest F1 Commission meeting |publisher=Formula One Management |date=21 September 2026 |access-date=21 September 2026}}
===Sporting regulations===
Due to changes to the fuel flow regulations for 2027, all races except for the [[Monaco Grand Prix]] will have a distance of {{cvt|290|km|mi|abbr=on}} instead of {{cvt|305|km|mi|abbr=on}}. This change will be implemented for the 2027 season only; from 2028 the distance will revert back to 305 km. {{cite news |title=F1 races to return to standard distance in 2028 |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/formula1/articles/c69wzvr14xlxo |publisher=BBC News |date=21 October 2026 |access-date=26 October 2026}}
The FIA will gain more flexibility in extending free practice sessions in case of interruptions. Additionally, the three hour clock which was used to determine the length of a race when including stoppages has been scrapped and will be replaced by a hard stop time for racing activity. The two hour race limit will remain the same.
