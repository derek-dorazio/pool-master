The '''2026 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament''' was a 68 team [[single-elimination tournament]] to determine the [[NCAA|National Collegiate Athletic Association]] (NCAA) [[NCAA Division I|Division I]] [[college basketball]] national champion for the [[2025–26 NCAA Division I women's basketball season]]. The 44th edition of the [[NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|tournament]] began on March 18, 2026, with the First Four, and concluded with the [[2026 NCAA Division I women's basketball championship game|championship game]] on April 5, at the [[Mortgage Matchup Center]] in [[Phoenix, Arizona]]. {{cite web |last1=Deatsch |first1=Thomas |title=2025 – 2031 Future Host Dates and Sites |url=https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/championships/sports/basketball/d1/women/2025-31D1WBB_FutureDatesAndSites.pdf |website=NCAA |access-date=24 March 2025 |date=2 October 2024}} The [[2025–26 UCLA Bruins women's basketball team|UCLA Bruins]] won their first national championship in the NCAA era by defeating the [[2025–26 South Carolina Gamecocks women's basketball team|South Carolina Gamecocks]] 79–51.
[[Coastal Athletic Association]] (CAA) champion [[2025–26 Charleston Cougars women's basketball team|Charleston]] was the only program in the field to make its NCAA tournament debut. With just one tournament debutant, the 2026 NCAA Tournament set the record for the fewest tournament debutants (the previous low was two, in the [[1998 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|1998]] and [[2017 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2017]] editions).
[[Atlantic 10 Conference|Atlantic 10]] champion [[2025–26 Rhode Island Rams women's basketball team|Rhode Island]] made its second-ever appearance and its first since [[1996 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|1996]]. [[Mid-American Conference|MAC]] champion [[2025–26 Miami RedHawks women's basketball team|Miami (OH)]] made its second-ever appearance and its first since [[2008 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2008]]. [[American Conference (NCAA)|American]] champion [[2025–26 UTSA Roadrunners women's basketball team|UTSA]] made its third-ever appearance and its first time since [[2009 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2009]]. [[Southern Conference|Southern]] champion [[2025–26 Samford Bulldogs women's basketball team|Samford]] made its third-ever appearance, its first since [[2012 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2012]]. [[Atlantic Sun Conference|Atlantic Sun]] champion [[2025–26 Jacksonville Dolphins women's basketball team|Jacksonville]] made its third-ever appearance, its first since [[2016 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2016]]. [[Ohio Valley Conference|Ohio Valley]] champion [[2025–26 Western Illinois Leathernecks women's basketball team|Western Illinois]] made its third-ever appearance, its first since [[2017 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2017]].
The Final Four featured the same four teams as the [[2025 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2025 tournament]], [[2025–26 UConn Huskies women's basketball team|UConn]] (who went in undefeated), South Carolina, [[2025–26 Texas Longhorns women's basketball team|Texas]], and UCLA. For only the fifth time in the tournament's history, all four of the number one seeds made it to the Final Four ([[1989 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|1989]], [[2012 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2012]], [[2015 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2015]], [[2018 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament|2018]]).
==Tournament procedure==
{{further|NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament#Current tournament format}}
A total of 68 teams were invited to participate in the 2026 tournament, consisting of the 31 [[List of NCAA conferences|conference]] champions and 37 "at-large" bids that were determined by the NCAA Selection Committee. The last four at-large teams and teams seeded 65 through 68 overall competed in First Four games, whose winners advanced to the 64-team first round. {{Cite web |date=17 November 2018 |title=Expansion of 2022 DI women's basketball tournament to 68 teams approved |url=https://www.ncaa.org/about/resources/media-center/news/general-expansion-of-2022-di-womens-basketball-tournament-to-68-teams-approved |access-date=24 March 2025 |website=NCAA |language=en |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211202065206/https://www.ncaa.org/about/resources/media-center/news/general-expansion-of-2022-di-womens-basketball-tournament-to-68-teams-approved |archive-date=2 December 2021 |url-status=dead}}
The top 16 seeds hosted the First Four, first round, and second round at their home arenas. In a procedural change, new for this tournament, the top 16 seeds were announced in alphabetical order on Saturday, March 15, ahead of the full bracket reveal that was announced on Sunday, March 16. This was done to give host sites more time to prepare and organize the logistics required to host. https://apnews.com/article/march-madness-ncaa-top-16-7e4359b396f0b86f3313d4c74bb4e918 https://espnpressroom.com/us/press-releases/2026/03/espn-returns-as-the-exclusive-home-of-the-ncaa-division-i-womens-basketball-bracket-reveal-espn-presents-wall-to-wall-surrounding-coverage-for-2026-selection-sunday/

The only upset in this tournament occurred when No. 2 seed Iowa lost at home to No. 10 seed Virginia in two overtimes in the round of 32.

===Television===
[[ESPN College Basketball|ESPN]] had the rights to broadcast each of the games for this tournament across [[ESPN]], [[ESPN2]], [[ESPNU]], [[ESPNEWS]], and [[ESPN on ABC|ABC]].
