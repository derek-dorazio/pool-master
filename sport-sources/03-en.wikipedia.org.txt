The '''2026 Stanley Cup playoffs''' was the [[Stanley Cup playoffs|playoff tournament]] of the [[National Hockey League]] (NHL) for the [[2025–26 NHL season|2025–26 season]]. The playoffs began on April 18, 2026, and concluded on June 14, 2026, with the [[Carolina Hurricanes]] winning their second [[Stanley Cup]] in franchise history, defeating the [[Vegas Golden Knights]] four games to two in the [[2026 Stanley Cup Final|Stanley Cup Final]]. {{cite web |url=https://media.nhl.com/site/asset/public/ext/2025-26/2025-26KeyDates.pdf |title=2025–26 Key Dates |publisher=National Hockey League |date=September 9, 2025 |access-date=March 21, 2026}}
The [[Colorado Avalanche]] qualified for the playoffs as the [[Presidents' Trophy]] winners with the most points (i.e., best record) during the regular season. The [[Buffalo Sabres]] ended their NHL-record 14-year playoff drought, clinching a playoff spot for the first time since [[2011 Stanley Cup playoffs|2011]]. The [[Anaheim Ducks]] qualified for the playoffs for the first time since [[2018 Stanley Cup playoffs|2018]], ending their seven-year playoff drought. The [[Philadelphia Flyers]] also ended their five-year playoff drought by making the playoffs for the first time since [[2020 Stanley Cup playoffs|2020]]. The [[Toronto Maple Leafs]] failed to qualify for the Stanley Cup playoffs for the first time since the [[2015–16 NHL season|2015–16 season]], ending what was the longest playoff streak in the NHL. The longest active playoff streak is now held jointly by the [[Colorado Avalanche]] and [[Tampa Bay Lightning]], who have qualified for the playoffs every year since 2018. Additionally, the two-time defending champion [[Florida Panthers]] failed to qualify for the playoffs, becoming the first defending champions to fail to qualify since the [[2014–15 Los Angeles Kings season|2014–15 Los Angeles Kings]], and the first two-time defending champions to miss the playoffs since the [[1969–70 Montreal Canadiens season|1969–70 Montreal Canadiens]]. {{cite web |last=Ganzi |first=Joey |date=2026-04-08 |title=Panthers Can Benefit From Missing the Playoffs This Season |url=https://www.yardbarker.com/nhl/articles/panthers_can_benefit_from_missing_the_playoffs_this_season/s1_16448_43702617 |access-date=2026-04-18 |website=Yardbarker |language=en}} The [[Winnipeg Jets]] also became the fifth defending Presidents' Trophy winner to fail to qualify for the playoffs. This is also the first time in league history that consecutive defending Presidents' Trophy winners failed to make the playoffs, as the New York Rangers failed to make the 2025 Stanley Cup Playoffs after winning the Presidents’ Trophy in 2024. The [[Utah Mammoth]] tied the [[Seattle Kraken]] as the second-fastest expansion team to clinch their first playoff berth, doing so in their second season, and for the first time since [[1965–66 NHL season#Playoffs|1966]], none of the [[New York metropolitan area]]-based teams (the [[New Jersey Devils]], [[New York Islanders]], and [[New York Rangers]]) qualified for the playoffs. {{cite web |last=Campbell |first=Ken |url=https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/look-ma-no-playoff-teams-what-went-wrong-in-new-york-and-who-will-fix-it |title=Look, Ma, No Playoff Teams! What Went Wrong In New York And Who Will Fix It? |work=[[The Hockey News]] |date=April 13, 2026 |access-date=April 13, 2026}}
For the first time since [[2019 Stanley Cup playoffs|2019]], following Tampa Bay's elimination in the first round, neither of the NHL's Florida-based teams made it to the Stanley Cup Final. The Carolina Hurricanes became the first team to have a 12–1 record going into the Final, and the fewest number of losses since the [[1975–76 Montreal Canadiens season|1975–76 Montreal Canadiens]]. {{cite news|url=https://nsjonline.com/article/2026/05/hurricanes-finish-off-canadiens-advance-to-stanley-cup-final-with-6-1-win/|title=Hurricanes finish off Canadiens, advance to Stanley Cup final with 6-1 win|date=May 30, 2026|newspaper=North State Journal|accessdate=May 29, 2026}}
The Hurricanes finished the playoffs with a 16–3 record, the fewest amount of losses for a Stanley Cup winner since the [[1987–88 Edmonton Oilers season|1987–88 Edmonton Oilers]]. {{cite magazine|url=https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/historic-16-3-carolina-hurricanes-shut-out-vegas-to-win-stanley-cup|title=Historic 16-3 Carolina Hurricanes Shut Out Vegas To Win Stanley Cup|last=Kennedy|first=Ryan|date=June 14, 2026|magazine=The Hockey News|accessdate=June 14, 2026}}

==Playoff bracket==
In each round, teams competed in a best-of-seven series following a 2–2–1–1–1 format (scores in the bracket indicate the number of games won in each best-of-seven series). The team with [[home advantage|home ice advantage]] plays at home for games one and two (and games five and seven, if necessary), and the other team plays at home for games three and four (and game six, if necessary). The top three teams in each division made the playoffs, along with two wild cards in each conference, for a total of eight teams from each conference.
In the first round, the lower [[seed (sports)|seeded]] wild card in the conference played against the division winner with the best record while the other wild card played against the other division winner, and both wild cards were [[de facto]] #4 seeds. The other series matched the second and third-place teams from the divisions. In the first two rounds, home-ice advantage was awarded to the team with the better seed. Thereafter, it was awarded to the team that had the better regular season record.

==First round==
===Eastern Conference first round===
====(A1) Buffalo Sabres vs. (WC1) Boston Bruins====
The Buffalo Sabres finished first in the Atlantic Division with 109 points. The Boston Bruins earned 100 points to finish as the first wild card in the Eastern Conference. This was the ninth playoff meeting between these two teams with Boston winning six of the eight previous series. They last met in the [[2010 Stanley Cup playoffs#(3) Buffalo Sabres vs. (6) Boston Bruins|2010 Eastern Conference quarterfinals]], which Boston won in six games. The Sabres qualified for the playoffs for the first time since [[2011 Stanley Cup playoffs|2011]]. Boston won three of the four games in the regular season series.
The Sabres defeated the Bruins in six games. ====(A2) Tampa Bay Lightning vs. (A3) Montreal Canadiens====
The Tampa Bay Lightning finished second in the Atlantic Division with 106 points. The Montreal Canadiens also earned 106 points to finish third in the Atlantic Division as Tampa Bay won the first tie-breaker of regulation wins. This was the fifth playoff meeting between these two teams with Tampa Bay winning three of the four previous series. They last met in the [[2021 Stanley Cup Final]], which Tampa Bay won in five games. These teams split their four-game regular season series.
The Canadiens defeated the Lightning in seven games, with each game-winning goal scored in the third period or overtime. In addition, each game was decided by one goal, the first time in a seven-game series since [[2015 Stanley Cup playoffs|2015]] and the third overall. ====(P1) Vegas Golden Knights vs. (WC1) Utah Mammoth====
The Vegas Golden Knights finished first in the Pacific Division with 95 points. The Utah Mammoth earned 92 points to finish as the first wild card in the Western Conference. This is the first playoff meeting between these two teams and the first playoff series for the Mammoth. Utah won two of the three games in the regular season series.
The Golden Knights defeated the Mammoth in six games. In game one, [[Carter Hart]] made 31 saves for the Golden Knights, who defeated the Mammoth 4–2. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/utah-mammoth-vegas-golden-knights-game-1-recap-april-19-2026|title=Golden Knights rally in 3rd, defeat Mammoth in Game 1 of West 1st Round|last=Delos Santos|first=Paul|date=April 20, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 20, 2026}} [[Kailer Yamamoto]] provided two assists in Utah's 3–2 victory in game two, tying the series 1–1 and giving the Mammoth their first playoff victory. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/utah-mammoth-vegas-golden-knights-game-2-recap-april-21-2026|title=Cooley breaks tie late in 3rd, Mammoth edge Golden Knights in Game 2 to even West 1st Round|last=Delos Santos|first=Paul|date=April 21, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 21, 2026}} In game three, [[Lawson Crouse]] scored twice giving Utah their first home playoff victory with a 4–2 triumph. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/vegas-golden-knights-utah-mammoth-game-3-recap-april-24-2026|title=Crouse scores twice, Mammoth defeat Golden Knights in Game 3 of West 1st Round|last=Komma|first=Matt|date=April 24, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 25, 2026}} In game four, [[Jack Eichel]]'s goal for Vegas was overturned in overtime due to an offside call. ====(P2) Edmonton Oilers vs. (P3) Anaheim Ducks====
The Edmonton Oilers finished second in the Pacific Division with 93 points. The Anaheim Ducks earned 92 points to finish third in the Pacific. This was the third playoff meeting between these two teams with both teams splitting the two previous series. They last met in the [[2017 Stanley Cup playoffs#(P1) Anaheim Ducks vs. (P2) Edmonton Oilers|2017 Western Conference second round]], which Anaheim won in seven games. The Ducks qualified for the playoffs for the first time since [[2018 Stanley Cup playoffs|2018]]. Edmonton won two of the three games in the regular season series.
The Ducks defeated the Oilers in six games. [[Kasperi Kapanen]] spoiled the Ducks' comeback in game one, scoring with 1:54 left in the game to make it 4–3 for the Oilers. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/anaheim-ducks-edmonton-oilers-game-1-recap-april-20-2026|title=Kapanen scores 2nd goal late, Oilers rally past Ducks in Game 1|last=Moddejonge|first=Gerry|date=April 20, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 20, 2026}} In game two, [[Jackson LaCombe]] provided three assists for the Ducks, defeating the Oilers 6–4. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/anaheim-ducks-edmonton-oilers-game-2-recap-april-22-2026|title=Gauthier breaks tie late in 3rd, Ducks edge Oilers in Game 2 to even West 1st Round|last=Moddejonge|first=Gerry|date=April 22, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 22, 2026}} [[Mikael Granlund]] scored a goal and assisted thrice in game three, giving the Ducks a 7–4 victory. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/edmonton-oilers-anaheim-ducks-game-3-recap-april-24-2026|title=Ducks score 4 in 3rd period, pull away from Oilers in Game 3 of Western 1st Round|last=Arritt|first=Dan|date=April 25, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 25, 2026}} In game four, the Ducks came back from a two-goal deficit to force overtime. After the video review, Poehling's goal stood and the Ducks took a 3–1 series lead with a 4–3 victory. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/edmonton-oilers-anaheim-ducks-game-4-recap-april-26-2026|title=Ducks rally to win Game 4 in OT, push Oilers to brink in West 1st Round|last=Arritt|first=Dan|date=April 26, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 28, 2026}} {{cite news|url=https://www.sfchronicle.com/sports/article/nhl-commissioner-bettman-backs-ruling-that-22231090.php|title=NHL Commissioner Bettman backs ruling that allowed Ducks' OT goal to stand in Game 4 win vs. Oilers|via=Associated Press|date=April 28, 2026|newspaper=San Francisco Chronicle|accessdate=April 28, 2026}} The Oilers avoided in elimination in game five as [[Leon Draisaitl]] scored twice in a 4–1 affair. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/anaheim-ducks-edmonton-oilers-game-5-recap-april-28-2026|title=Draisaitl scores twice, Oilers stave off elimination with Game 5 win against Ducks|last=Moddejonge|first=Gerry|date=April 28, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 30, 2026}} [[Troy Terry]] and [[Chris Kreider]] provided a goal and two assists in game six, giving Anaheim a 5–2 victory and its first playoff series win since [[2017 Stanley Cup playoffs|2017]]. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/edmonton-oilers-anaheim-ducks-game-6-recap-april-30-2026|title=Ducks win Game 6, eliminate Oilers in Western 1st Round|last=Arritt|first=Dan|date=April 30, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=April 30, 2026}}

==Conference finals==
{{Main|NHL conference finals}}
===Eastern Conference final===
====(M1) Carolina Hurricanes vs. (A3) Montreal Canadiens====
This was the eighth playoff meeting between these two teams, with Montreal winning five of the seven previous series. They last met in the [[2006 Stanley Cup playoffs#(2) Carolina Hurricanes vs. (7) Montreal Canadiens|2006 Eastern Conference quarterfinals]], which Carolina won in six games. Carolina made their second consecutive and seventh overall conference finals appearance. Carolina lost the [[2025 Stanley Cup playoffs#Eastern Conference final|previous year's]] Eastern Conference final against the [[Florida Panthers]], in five games. This was Montreal's ninth appearance in the semifinal/conference finals since the league began using a 16-team or greater playoff format in [[1979–80 NHL season|1980]]. Their most recent appearance was in the [[2021 Stanley Cup playoffs#(1) Vegas Golden Knights vs. (4) Montreal Canadiens|2021 semifinals]], which Montreal won against the [[Vegas Golden Knights]] in six games. Montreal won all three games in the regular season series.
Carolina defeated the Canadiens in five games. [[Juraj Slafkovský]] scored twice in Montreal's 6–2 rout of Carolina in game one, with the Canadiens scoring four goals in the first period. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/montreal-canadiens-carolina-hurricanes-game-1-recap-may-21-2026|title=Canadiens score 4 in 1st period, defeat Hurricanes in Game 1 of East Final|last=Dusterberg|first=Kurt|date=May 21, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 25, 2026}} The Hurricanes then tied the series in game two with [[Nikolaj Ehlers]]'s overtime goal, his second goal of the game, winning 3–2. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/montreal-canadiens-carolina-hurricanes-game-2-recap-may-23-2026|title=Ehlers scores in OT, Hurricanes top Canadiens in Game 2 to even East Final|last=Dusterberg|first=Kurt|date=May 23, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 25, 2026}} In game three, [[Andrei Svechnikov]] scored for Carolina in overtime, giving the Hurricanes a 3–2 victory. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/carolina-hurricanes-montreal-canadiens-game-3-recap-may-25-2026|title=Svechnikov's OT goal lifts Hurricanes past Canadiens in Game 3 of East Final|last=Farrell|first=Sean|date=May 25, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 25, 2026}} The Hurricanes shut out the Canadiens 4–0 in game four with [[Frederik Andersen]] stopping all 18 shots he faced. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/carolina-hurricanes-montreal-canadiens-game-4-recap-may-27-2026|title=Hurricanes shut out Canadiens in Game 4, move 1 win from Stanley Cup Final|last=Farrell|first=Sean|date=May 27, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 29, 2026}} In game five, [[Logan Stankoven]] provided a goal and two assists for the Hurricanes, defeating Montreal 6–1 to advance to the Stanley Cup Final. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/montreal-canadiens-carolina-hurricanes-game-5-recap-may-29-2026|title=Hurricanes cruise past Canadiens in Game 5 to reach Stanley Cup Final|last=Dusterberg|first=Kurt|date=May 29, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 29, 2026}}

===Western Conference final===
====(C1) Colorado Avalanche vs. (P1) Vegas Golden Knights====
This was the second playoff series between these two teams. Their only previous series was in the [[2021 Stanley Cup playoffs#(W1) Colorado Avalanche vs. (W2) Vegas Golden Knights|2021 West Division second round]], which Vegas won in six games. This was Vegas's fifth semifinals/conference finals appearance. Their last appearance was in [[2023 Stanley Cup playoffs#(P1) Vegas Golden Knights vs. (C2) Dallas Stars|2023]] against the [[Dallas Stars]], which Vegas won in six games. This was Colorado's tenth appearance in the conference final. They last made the conference final in [[2022 Stanley Cup playoffs#Western Conference final|2022]], which Colorado won against the [[Edmonton Oilers]] in a four-game sweep. Colorado won two of the three games in this year's regular season series.
The Golden Knights defeated the Avalanche in a four-game sweep. In game one, [[Carter Hart]] made 36 saves for the Golden Knights, holding off the Avalanche for a 4–2 victory. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/vegas-golden-knights-colorado-avalanche-game-1-recap-may-20-2026|title=Golden Knights hold off Avalanche in Game 1 of Western Final|last=Boulding|first=Ryan|date=May 20, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 25, 2026}} [[Ivan Barbashev]] scored twice and provided an assist for the Golden Knights as Hart made 30 saves in game two, backstopping Vegas to a 3–1 victory. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/vegas-golden-knights-colorado-avalanche-game-2-recap-may-22-2026|title=Golden Knights rally past Avalanche to win Game 2, extend West Final lead|last=Boulding|first=Ryan|date=May 22, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 25, 2026}} The Golden Knights came back from a three-goal deficit in game three, taking a 3–0 series lead with a 5–3 triumph. {{cite web|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/colorado-avalanche-vegas-golden-knights-game-3-recap-may-24-2026|title=Golden Knights erase 3-goal deficit, on verge of sweeping Avalanche in Western Final|last=Delos Santos|first=Paul|date=May 24, 2026|website=NHL.com|accessdate=May 25, 2026}} In game four, Vegas held off the Avalanche in the third period, giving them a 2–1 victory, and a Stanley Cup Final berth. With the series victory, the Golden Knights became the first team to record a sweep over a [[Presidents' Trophy]]-winning team since the [[Columbus Blue Jackets]] in [[2019 Tampa Bay Lightning–Columbus Blue Jackets playoff series|2019]]. {{cite web |date=2026-05-27 |title=Golden Knights sweep Avalanche, advance to Stanley Cup Final {{!}} NHL.com |url=https://www.nhl.com/news/colorado-avalanche-vegas-golden-knights-game-4-recap-may-26-2026 |access-date=2026-05-27 |website=www.nhl.com |language=en}} This was the only series of the playoffs not to have a game go into overtime.

==Media==
===Canada===
This was the 12th and final postseason of the league's Canadian national broadcast rights deal with [[Rogers Sports & Media]] before a new 12-season contract begins next season (2026–27). {{cite web|last=Rosen|first=Dan|title=NHL, Rogers announce landmark 12-year deal|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-rogers-announce-landmark-12-year-deal/c-693152|access-date=August 24, 2019|website=NHL.com|date=November 26, 2013|archive-date=June 20, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190620135912/https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-rogers-announce-landmark-12-year-deal/c-693152|url-status=dead}} {{cite web|title=NHL, Rogers announce 12-year national rights media deal|url=https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-rogers-announce-12-year-rights-deal|access-date=April 2, 2025|date=April 2, 2025|archive-date=April 2, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250402231513/https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-rogers-announce-12-year-rights-deal|url-status=live}} Shortly after the 2026 Stanley Cup Final concluded, the [[Canadian Broadcasting Corporation|CBC]] announced that it would no longer sublicense NHL games from Rogers, making game six the last NHL game to air on [[CBC Television]]. {{cite web |date=June 16, 2026|title=CBC will no longer show NHL games as sublicense with rights-holder Rogers Sportsnet ends|url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/cbc-nhl-hockey-night-in-canada-ends-9.7236977|access-date=June 16, 2026|website=CBC Sports}}
Games aired across [[NHL on Sportsnet|Sportsnet]], [[Sportsnet One|SN1]], [[Sportsnet 360|SN360]], and CBC under the ''[[Hockey Night in Canada]]'' brand. For first and second-round U.S.–U.S. games not on CBC, Sportsnet used some the U.S. feed instead of producing their own telecast. The 2026 Stanley Cup Final was simulcast on both CBC and Sportsnet. {{cite press release|title=Sportsnet Announces 2026 Stanley Cup Playoffs First Round Coverage Details, Beginning April 18|url=https://www.rogerssportsandmedia.com/the-latest/sportsnet-announces-2026-stanley-cup-playoffs-first-round-coverage-details-beginning-april-18/|website=Rogers Sports and Media|date=April 17, 2026}}
[[Sportsnet+]] streamed all games.
===United States===
This was the fifth season of the league's seven-year U.S. national broadcast rights deals with the [[NHL on ESPN|ESPN family of networks]] and [[NHL on TNT|TNT Sports]]. {{cite news|title=NHL moving to Turner Sports is $1 billion risk-reward for hockey|work=CNBC|url=https://www.cnbc.com/2021/04/27/turner-sports-nhl-announce-seven-year-deal-for-broadcast-rights.html|access-date=April 27, 2021|date=April 27, 2021|archive-date=November 4, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221104084621/https://www.cnbc.com/2021/04/27/turner-sports-nhl-announce-seven-year-deal-for-broadcast-rights.html|url-status=live}}
First- and second-round games were split between ESPN-produced telecasts (either on ESPN, [[NHL on ABC|ABC]], or [[ESPN2]]) and TNT Sports-produced telecasts (either on TNT or [[TBS (American TV channel)|TBS]], with selected simulcasts on [[TruTV]]). Each U.S. team's regional broadcaster also televised local coverage of first-round games. {{cite press release|title=2026 Stanley Cup Playoffs presented by GEICO begin Saturday on ESPN|url=https://espnpressroom.com/us/press-releases/2026/04/2026-stanley-cup-playoffs-presented-by-geico-begin-saturday-on-espn/|website=ESPN Press Room|date=April 18, 2026}} {{cite press release |title=TNT Sports Announces Commentators for 2026 Stanley Cup Playoffs Presented by GEICO First Round Games on Sunday, April 19 |url=https://press.wbd.com/us/media-release/tnt-sports-announces-commentators-2026-stanley-cup-playoffs-presented-geico-first |website=wbd.com |date=April 18, 2026}}
ESPN/ABC had the first choice of which conference final series to air, choosing the Western Conference finals this season. As a result, TNT Sports broadcast the Eastern Conference finals. {{cite web |title=Conference Final schedule scenarios announced | url=https://www.nhl.com/news/start-times-for-conference-final-scenarios-announced |website=www.nhl.com |access-date=May 14, 2026}} As per the alternating rotation, ABC has coverage of the 2026 Stanley Cup Final.
All ESPN games streamed on the [[ESPN app]]'s unlimited tier, while TNT games streamed on [[HBO Max]].
